# UN puts pandemic preparedness back on the agenda: what countries agreed

_In brief:_ **At a UN General Assembly high-level meeting, countries renewed commitments to prevent and prepare for pandemics. The agenda centers on sustained financing, early detection, resilient health systems, and equitable access to vaccines, diagnostics, and treatment. The next major step is expected at the World Health Assembly in May 2027.**

On September 25, the UN General Assembly held its second high-level meeting on pandemic prevention, preparedness, and response. Participants reviewed progress since the first meeting in 2023 and agreed that preparedness must remain a standing political priority instead of returning to the agenda only during the next crisis.

## What countries agreed at the meeting

According to the World Health Organization, countries supported a political declaration built around equity, solidarity, international cooperation, and long-term investment. The document is expected to go to the General Assembly for further consideration.

In practical terms, the agenda links several tasks:

- detect dangerous outbreaks earlier and exchange verified data faster;
- strengthen routine national health systems, including laboratories and workforce training;
- maintain financing and infrastructure between crises;
- expand local production of vaccines, medicines, and diagnostics;
- provide timely access to those tools for countries at different income levels;
- apply the One Health approach to the links among human, animal, and environmental health.

The declaration sets political direction. Results will depend on national budgets, legislation, compatible surveillance systems, and countries’ capacity to carry out their commitments.

## How this connects to the WHO Pandemic Agreement

The international framework has changed over the past three years. Countries adopted the WHO Pandemic Agreement, and amended International Health Regulations entered into force. A critical part of the agreement is still under negotiation: the annex on Pathogen Access and Benefit Sharing, known as PABS.

PABS is intended to set rules for timely sharing of samples and data from pathogens with pandemic potential, as well as the distribution of vaccines, medicines, and diagnostics developed through that exchange. WHO expects the annex to go before the World Health Assembly in May 2027. Countries could then begin their national consideration of ratification.

Until the annex is agreed, the international framework cannot be considered complete. The negotiations sit at the intersection of rapid scientific exchange, sovereign control of biological material, and fair access to the products of research.

## Why the issue is urgent again

COVID-19 demonstrated that medical readiness affects mortality, education, employment, transport, and public finances at the same time. WHO links the pandemic to more than 20 million excess deaths between 2020 and 2023 and more than US$10 trillion in economic losses.

International agreements cannot prevent a new pathogen from emerging. Their purpose is to shorten the path from the first signal to verification, containment, and production of necessary tools. The earlier that chain works, the lower the risk that a local outbreak becomes a global crisis.

## What this may mean for Kazakhstan

The UN and WHO releases describe global commitments and do not set out a separate new plan for Kazakhstan. Our editorial inference is that preparedness can be assessed through measurable elements: laboratory turnaround time, data exchange between medical and veterinary services, reserves of protective equipment, hospital resilience, and procedures for informing the public.

Two levels of decisions now matter: final General Assembly consideration of the political declaration and PABS negotiations leading to May 2027. Concrete mechanisms for financing, data access, and allocation of medical products will show whether the political promise has become an operating system.
