# FAA assessment in Kazakhstan ends: what it means for direct US flights

_In brief:_ **Experts from the US Federal Aviation Administration have completed an assessment of Kazakhstan’s state aviation safety oversight system. The preliminary stage is complete, but Category 1 has not yet been granted. We explain what the IASA program examines, what happens next, and why this is an important but not final condition for direct flights to the United States.**

From September 21 to 25, experts from the US Federal Aviation Administration conducted an International Aviation Safety Assessment, or IASA, in Kazakhstan. They presented preliminary findings at a final oral briefing. A formal decision on the country’s status will come later.

## What the FAA experts assessed

IASA does not rate an individual aircraft or the quality of passenger service. It examines whether a country’s civil aviation system can oversee its airlines in accordance with the standards and recommended practices of the International Civil Aviation Organization, ICAO.

The FAA identifies eight critical elements of effective oversight:

1. primary aviation legislation;
2. specific operating regulations;
3. the state civil aviation system and safety oversight functions;
4. technical personnel qualifications and training;
5. technical guidance, tools, and safety-critical information;
6. licensing, certification, authorization, and approval obligations;
7. surveillance obligations;
8. resolution of safety concerns.

Kazakhstan’s Aviation Administration said the experts reviewed legislation, implementing regulations, and the state’s practical ability to conduct oversight. On September 24, the FAA team also visited Air Astana in Astana. The airline is applying for economic authority from the US Department of Transportation to operate services to the United States.

## What happens next

After the onsite assessment, the FAA is expected to send a formal notice to Kazakhstan’s aviation authorities. Final consultations will follow. If shortcomings were identified, the parties will discuss corrective actions. The US regulator will decide Kazakhstan’s category only after that process.

At the preliminary briefing, the experts noted the work already completed and good results in several areas. That is a positive signal, but an oral briefing is not the final determination and does not disclose the full set of possible findings.

## What Category 1 means

Category 1 means that a country’s state oversight system complies with applicable international aviation safety standards. The IASA category concerns the government system rather than a specific airline.

Category 1 would create the regulatory conditions for Kazakh carriers to proceed through further US processes for direct service or code sharing with US airlines. It would not open a route automatically. Separate authorizations, an airline’s commercial decision, aircraft and crew availability, airport slots, and operational compliance would still be required.

The accurate description at this stage is that Kazakhstan has completed an assessment that may bring direct service closer. The final category and a launch date for flights have not been announced.

## Why the outcome matters without an immediate route

If IASA produces findings, they will identify specific parts of the oversight system that need strengthening. Corrective work affects more than a future US route. The quality of legislation, inspector training, certification, and surveillance applies to the country’s entire civil aviation system.

For passengers, the next verifiable milestone is the FAA’s publication of a formal decision. Until then, any precise date for direct flights should be treated as a plan rather than a confirmed schedule.
