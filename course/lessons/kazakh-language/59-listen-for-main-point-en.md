# Gist first, details second: listen to an announcement

_Lead (summary):_ **You will catch an announcement's purpose on the first listen and extract time and action on the second.**

## A real situation

A fictional library records an announcement for project participants. It is short, but trying to transcribe every word at once may hide the main change.

## Listen and respond

[Listen: Назар аударыңыздар, кітапхана бүгін ерте жабылады.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-329.wav) — Attention: the library closes early today.

[Listen: Оқу залы сағат төртке дейін ашық.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-330.wav) — The reading room is open until four.

[Listen: Кітаптарды сағат үшке дейін қайтарыңыздар.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-331.wav) — Please return books by three.

[Listen: Ертең кітапхана әдеттегідей жұмыс істейді.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-332.wav) — Tomorrow the library works as usual.

[Listen: Бүгін не өзгерді?](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-333.wav) — What changed today?

[Listen: Кітаптарды қашан қайтару керек?](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-334.wav) — When must the books be returned?

## How to practise

First play the recordings with the transcript covered and state only the main meaning. On a second pass note anchor words; on a third, check the written lines. Do not let reading replace listening. Repeat the whole phrase at a natural pace instead of spelling every letter aloud. If the first pass leaves a gap, keep the main point and fill one missing detail on the next pass.

## The precise model

On the first listen ask why the message exists: today's hours changed. On the second ask what you must do: return books by three, use the reading room until four. Do not merge these two deadlines.

## The support signal

```kazakh
НЕГІЗГІ ОЙ → ҚЫЗМЕТ → УАҚЫТ → ӘРЕКЕТ
```

## The limit of the rule

This is a teaching scenario, not the actual hours of a real library. `Дейін` sets a limit: 'until four' does not mean you can start at four.

## Predict before the explanation

Before reading the explanation, predict what the message is about and which fact your partner will need. Then compare your prediction with the recording and find one exact sound or word that changed your conclusion.

## Rebuild it without the prompt

Hide the text and map. Sketch four support nodes in one minute, say a line from memory, and tell a partner which conclusion follows from it and which would be a guess.

## Find and repair the mistake

A learner hears 'four' and assumes books can be returned until then. Separate the two services and correct the plan.

## Transfer to a new setting

Make a new notice with different return and closing times. The listener must keep the two deadlines apart.

## Change to the My World project

Add an audio-message card to My World 2.0: verified fact, unknown detail, follow-up question and confirmed answer. Use fictional names and locations. Create a fictional notice with two separate deadlines and a listening-check table.

## Exercise

Have a partner play the four lines in a mixed order. First state only the main change; on the second listen make a service/deadline table. Apply the method to an unseen announcement.

## Retrieval after 1, 7, and 30 days

- Tomorrow listen once and reconstruct the four nodes without text.
- After seven days use the technique with a new recording containing a different time, place or object.
- After thirty days retell what you heard to someone else and ask them to check its accuracy.
