# Six more is not twice as many: compare with a baseline

_Lead (summary):_ **You will describe an absolute difference and a relative change while naming the baseline.**

## A real-life scene

The fictional library table shows 12 people on Monday and 18 on Tuesday. One learner says “six more”; another says “twice as many.” Check both before writing a chart caption.

## Listen and respond

[Listen: Сейсенбіде алты адамға көп келді.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-383.wav) — Six more people came on Tuesday.

[Listen: Бастапқы сан — он екі.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-384.wav) — The baseline is twelve.

[Listen: Он сегіз он екіден бір жарым есе көп.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-385.wav) — Eighteen is one and a half times twelve.

[Listen: Екі есе болса, жиырма төрт болар еді.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-386.wav) — Twice twelve would be twenty-four.

[Listen: Өсім бастапқы санның жартысына тең.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-387.wav) — The increase is half of the starting number.

[Listen: Негіз өзгерсе, пайыз да өзгереді.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-388.wav) — Change the baseline and the percentage changes.

## How to practise

First listen with the transcript hidden and state the main point even if you miss some words. On a second pass write only anchor words and numbers; on a third, open the text and find one error in your initial prediction. Repeat the full Kazakh sentence at a natural pace rather than spelling it out. If a detail remains unclear, use the repair question from lesson 60 and ask a partner to repeat just that part.

## The precise model

Compute `18 − 12 = 6`. For growth relative to Monday, divide six by **Monday’s** 12: one half, or 50%. Tuesday is 150% of Monday, or 1.5 times as many. The reverse drop from 18 to 12 is six out of 18, about 33%, not 50%. In speech, name both days and the baseline.

## Support signal

```kazakh
НЕГІЗ → АЙЫРМА → ҮЛЕС → ДҰРЫС СӨЗ
```

## Limit of the rule

Percentages help only when the baseline and comparable observations are clear. These fictional data say nothing about a real library’s attendance.

## Predict before the explanation

Before reading the model, write the conclusion you expect and what you can actually verify. Name one word or number that might change your answer. Compare your prediction with the evidence afterward; revising it is a sign of careful reading, not failure.

## Recall without the support

Hide the text and diagram. Sketch four nodes in one minute, say two connected Kazakh sentences, and explain to a partner where the known information ends. Reopen the page, compare every detail and repair one imprecise word.

## Find and repair the mistake

“18 is twice 12” is false. Calculate what twice 12 would be and replace the sentence.

## Transfer to a new scene

Now compare Wednesday (15) with Tuesday (18). Name the absolute change and baseline; do not reuse yesterday’s 50% for a new pair.

## Change to the My World project

My World 2.1 remains a fictional learning guide without personal data. For every conclusion keep the original message, the date you checked it, and what you still do not know. Add two captions: “+6 versus Monday” and “+50% versus Monday.” Show the arithmetic beside them.

## Exercise

Calculate `18−12`, `6÷12` and `6÷18` on paper. Say “six more,” “up by half,” and “down by about a third” in Kazakh. Explain why the percentages differ.
