# May, must not, and need to: understanding rules

_Lead (summary):_ **You will distinguish permission, prohibition, and necessity at school, in a library, and on the street.**

## Where we are on the map

The support is a route rather than a poster to memorise. Find the starting point, trace the arrows, and name the meaning of each step. Then hide the map and rebuild it on a blank sheet.

## A real situation

Doors and corridors display signs without a translation. You must identify what is allowed, forbidden, or required for safety, then ask for permission politely.

## Listen and respond

[Listen: Кітапханада тыныш сөйлеу керек.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-121.wav) — You need to speak quietly in the library.

[Listen: Дәлізде жүгіруге болмайды.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-122.wav) — Running in the corridor is not allowed.

[Listen: Сыныпқа кіруге бола ма?](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-123.wav) — May I enter the classroom?

[Listen: Иә, кіруге болады.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-124.wav) — Yes, you may enter.

## How to practise

On the first listen, hide the text and identify only the situation. On the second, reveal the Kazakh line, point to its meaning units, and repeat after the recording. On the third, remove the model, change a place, time, or direction card, and produce a new sentence. The recording supplies a pronunciation model; compare your speech with it or ask a live speaker to check it.

## The precise model

An action takes a form such as `сөйлеуге`, `жүгіруге`, `кіруге`, or `өтуге`. A modal word then completes its status: `болады` means allowed, `болмайды` means not allowed, and `керек` means needed. The question particle is separate in `бола ма?` Identify the action first and the rule status second.

## The support signal

```kazakh
ӘРЕКЕТ + -УҒА/-УГЕ → болады / болмайды / керек
```

## The limit of the rule

`Керек` may express practical necessity, a rule, or advice; the situation determines the force. Do not turn lesson examples into universal legal claims. A pictogram, a staff instruction, and a formal regulation can carry different authority.

## Predict before the explanation

Before reading the rule, inspect the new cards and choose a form by meaning: where an action happens, where a route starts, where it ends, when an event starts, or which rule applies. Record the prediction, then explain which signal confirmed or changed it.

## Rebuild it without the prompt

Hide the page and draw the support from memory in sixty seconds. Use it to say three lines: the familiar model, one changed line, and one entirely your own. If a link is missing, reveal only that link, hide the page again, and rebuild the whole chain.

## Find and repair the mistake

A learner sees a quiet sign and says `сөйлеуге болмайды`, banning all speech. If the sign asks for quiet speech, the precise line is `тыныш сөйлеу керек`. Separate prohibition of an action from a requirement to change how it is done.

## Transfer to a new setting

Change the situation rather than replacing a single word: use another school, day, time, route, or rule. A partner supplies one unseen card and checks whether your ending still matches the intended meaning.

## Change to the My World project

Add six clear signs to the school and city map: two permissions, two prohibitions, and two necessities. Every caption must match its image exactly.

## Exercise

**Required.** Sort nine cards under `болады`, `болмайды`, and `керек`, explaining three ambiguous cases. Ask two `бола ма?` questions. **Transfer check.** Receive a new sign and formulate its rule without copying a model line.
