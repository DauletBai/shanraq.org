# The Kazakh sentence skeleton: action closes the thought

_Lead (summary):_ **You will assemble a clear Kazakh sentence from who, detail, and action cards and see why a neutral statement commonly ends with its predicate.**

## Where we are on the map

The map holds the whole route; we will add its details one at a time.

## A real situation

Picture a short scene: a person picks up a book and reads. Russian or English may encourage another familiar order, while a neutral Kazakh line normally names the participant and details before placing the action at the end: `Мен кітап оқимын`. The final word closes the route of the thought.

## How to practise

Press the button and look away from the text first: catch the overall meaning and just one new sound or structural choice. On the second pass, follow the phrase and the support map with your finger. Hide the model, rebuild it from two or three signs, and say a version of your own. Finish with a partner or a voice recorder: respond without preparation and note one place where you paused. Return to that link only instead of rereading the whole lesson.

## The precise model

The basic skeleton is **who? → what, where, when, how? → does what?** In `Мен кітап оқимын`, `Мен` is the participant, `кітап` the object, and `оқимын` the action. In `Аружан мектепке барады`, the destination precedes `барады`. A describing word also precedes its noun: `жаңа кітап`. Separate a line into cards, then reconnect it in one voice without a pause after every word.

## The support signal

```kazakh
КІМ? → ДЕРЕК: НЕ? ҚАЙДА? ҚАШАН? → ӘРЕКЕТ
```

## The limit of the rule

This is a neutral order rather than an iron cage. A known participant can disappear in live speech, and a moved word can receive special focus or style. Ending an ordinary beginner statement with its action is a safe route. We are not deriving every verb ending yet; use the example forms as meaningful complete frames.

## Predict before the explanation

Before reading the explanation, assemble `оқимын / Мен / кітап` and `барады / мектепке / Аружан`. Point to the word that should close each neutral thought and explain its job without using the grammar term predicate.

## Rebuild it without the prompt

Hide the text and draw three slots: person, detail, action. Rebuild two heard sentences and replace only the middle card. If a new word would require an ending you have not learned, use a safe complete form from the lesson instead of guessing.

## Find and repair the mistake

A learner says `Мен оқимын кітап`, carrying over the order of another language. The meaning is guessable, but the neutral Kazakh construction is disturbed. Move the action card to the end, then explain why `Ол жаңа оқиды кітап` must also keep `жаңа кітап` together.

## Transfer to a new setting

Describe three observable actions at home, school, or a library without naming real people. Lay out the cards first and speak without them second. A partner should identify the action word and the position where they heard it.

## Change to the My World project

Add three safe lines to My World: the fictional guide character reads, goes somewhere, or works in the chosen place. Store a small who | detail | action analysis under each. Remove that analysis in the second version and test whether another person can reconstruct it.

## Exercise

**Required.** Build four sentences from the learned frames. Underline the participant once, the detail twice, and circle the action. Shuffle one sentence and restore it without the model.

**Transfer check.** Give a partner new cards containing familiar words. They assemble the line; you identify only the first obstacle without dictating a finished answer.
