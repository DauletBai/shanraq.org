# Mastery checkpoint 2.1: report data and defend a conclusion

_Lead (summary):_ **Without a script, you will read a new table, separate opinion from fact and defend a careful conclusion.**

## Where we are on the map

Trace the four nodes of the support map with a finger and name the job of each one. Then hide it. Colours make the checking sequence visible; they do not stand in for evidence. If a node lacks information, leave it blank and explain why. These figures are invented practice data, not statistics from a real library.

## A real-life scene

A fictional school library has exercise counts: Thursday 14, Friday 20, Saturday 16 visitors. Source: the group’s 7 October card; these are not real school data. A partner claims the library is always overcrowded on Fridays.

## Listen and respond

[Listen: Бейсенбіде он төрт адам келді.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-407.wav) — Fourteen people came on Thursday.

[Listen: Жұмада жиырма адам келді.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-408.wav) — Twenty people came on Friday.

[Listen: Сенбіде он алты адам келді.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-409.wav) — Sixteen people came on Saturday.

[Listen: Жұмадағы сан бейсенбіден алтыға көп.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-410.wav) — Friday’s count is six above Thursday’s.

[Listen: «Әр жұмада» деуге дерек жеткіліксіз.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-411.wav) — There is not enough evidence for “every Friday.”

[Listen: Орын санын бөлек тексеру керек.](/static/course/kazakh-language/audio/kz-412.wav) — We need to check the number of seats separately.

## How to practise

First listen with the transcript hidden and state the main point even if you miss some words. On a second pass write only anchor words and numbers; on a third, open the text and find one error in your initial prediction. Repeat the full Kazakh sentence at a natural pace rather than spelling it out. If a detail remains unclear, use the repair question from lesson 60 and ask a partner to repeat just that part.

## The precise model

First report the source, date, three counts and unit. Then compare Friday with Thursday: `20 − 14 = 6`, not “twice as many.” “This Friday exceeded this Thursday and Saturday” follows from the card. “Every Friday” and “overcrowded” need other evidence. Name the missing information and write a careful note.

## Support signal

```kazakh
ДЕРЕККӨЗ → ҮШ МӘН → ШЕК → ШЕШІМ
```

## Limit of the rule

This is a checkpoint using invented data, not a claim about a real school. Judge accurate listening, chart reading and reasoning rather than polished delivery.

## Predict before the explanation

Before reading the model, write the conclusion you expect and what you can actually verify. Name one word or number that might change your answer. Compare your prediction with the evidence afterward; revising it is a sign of careful reading, not failure.

## Recall without the support

Hide the text and diagram. Sketch four nodes in one minute, say two connected Kazakh sentences, and explain to a partner where the known information ends. Reopen the page, compare every detail and repair one imprecise word.

## Find and repair the mistake

A learner writes “attendance rose 50% and the room is full.” Check the base: six out of fourteen is about 43%; capacity is unknown.

## Transfer to a new scene

Your partner changes Saturday’s count to 23. Redraw the chart and revise only conclusions that depend on that number.

## Change to the My World project

My World 2.1 remains a fictional learning guide without personal data. For every conclusion keep the original message, the date you checked it, and what you still do not know. Assemble My World 2.1: a checkable card, chart with original counts, separate personal opinion, correction log and an unscripted oral defence.

## Exercise

Final task: hide the text, listen to six lines twice, draw three bars from zero, and state the supported conclusion in Kazakh. With a partner, disagree respectfully, then write a card with its source and an open question.

## Review after 1, 7 and 30 days

- Tomorrow listen to one line without text and recover its meaning and source.
- After seven days apply the same map to a new fictional notice or a fresh set of numbers.
- After thirty days explain your decision to someone else without the map and ask them to find an unsupported part.
