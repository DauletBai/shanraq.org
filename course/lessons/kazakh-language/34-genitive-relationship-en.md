# Whose part? Genitive as a bridge between two names

_Lead (summary):_ **You will connect an owner or whole to its part through the matching `кімнің/ненің? ↔ несі?` construction.**

## A real situation

A public-space plan labels a door, yard, map, and timetable in the same way. To tell which place each item belongs with, you build a two-sided relationship instead of attaching an ending only to the first word.

## How to practise

On the first listen, hide the text and identify the role of the highlighted word. On the second, reveal the Kazakh line, point to the stem and ending, and repeat the whole sentence. On the third, replace the place, object, or participant and choose the role again before choosing an ending. Use the recording for pronunciation and prove understanding with a new example.

## The precise model

The relationship has two banks. The first noun answers `кімнің/ненің?` and takes `-ның/-нің`, `-дың/-дің`, or `-тың/-тің`. The second names the related item and takes a third-person possessive ending, as in `ауласы`, `картасы`, `есігі`, and `дәптері`. In `досымның үйі`, `досым` first carries the personal link ‘my’ and then the genitive link to the home.

## The support signal

```kazakh
КІМНІҢ/НЕНІҢ? → мектептің ══ ауласы ← НЕСІ?
```

## The limit of the rule

A genitive construction does more than state legal ownership: it may express part-whole structure, authorship, or a relationship to a place or group. Do not infer a person's ownership from the form alone. Some established links may shorten in conversation, but a beginner benefits from building the complete, testable bridge first.

## Predict before the explanation

Before the explanation, act out the scene with objects. Ask who acts, whose part it is, what is specifically affected, to whom or where it goes, where it is, where it comes from, and with whom or by what it is done. Record the role and question, then predict the form. The rule checks your decision rather than replacing it.

## Rebuild it without the prompt

Hide the lesson and draw a three-object scene. Label roles without case names, then add questions and finally endings. Say the familiar sentence, a changed sentence, and your own. If a form disappears, return to the meaning arrow instead of mechanically trying every option.

## Find and repair the mistake

A learner writes `мектептің аула`, completing only the first bank. The listener expects a relationship signal on the second noun. Add `-сы` to make `мектептің ауласы`. Then hide the first word and use `ауласы` to recover that the yard belongs with a larger whole.

## Transfer to a new setting

Receive an unseen scene with another object, person, route, or means. Explain the form with one short reason: ‘this is the goal’, ‘this is the location’, or ‘this is the source’. A partner changes one role; change only the ending and meaning connected with that role.

## Change to the My World project

Add five privacy-safe links to the project: a district map, library door, school yard, club timetable, and an invented learner's item. Join each pair with the same type of line.

## Exercise

**Required.** Build six `кімнің/ненің? ↔ несі?` pairs and explain the type of relationship. **Transfer check.** A partner changes the second noun; rebuild both banks wherever the meaning requires it.
