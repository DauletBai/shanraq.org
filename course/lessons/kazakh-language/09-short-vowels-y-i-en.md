# Ы and І: a quiet sound is not a silent letter

_Lead (summary):_ **You will hear and produce Kazakh ы and і as very short vowels, avoid replacing them with strong familiar vowels, and recognise the particular forms in which a vowel really disappears.**

## A real situation

A new learner may see `ы` and turn it into a separate heavy syllable, or replace `і` with a long English or Russian-style vowel. The extra beat changes the rhythm of Kazakh. A native speaker often makes these vowels so short that a beginner thinks the letter is silent. Our task is to reduce the duration without deleting the sound.

## How to practise

Listen with the text hidden and catch only the target meaning. Next, trace the map with a finger and repeat after the model. On the third pass, hide the model, tap its short rhythm with your palm, and make a sentence of your own. Device speech is a model; use a speaker, teacher, or comparison with your own recording as evidence of pronunciation.

## The precise model

`Ы` is a close unrounded back or central vowel and `і` is its front partner. Modern descriptions analyse them as vowel phonemes, even though transcription varies. They preserve the back or front melody of a word and help select a suffix: `ыдыс-тар`, but `тіс-тер`. An apostrophe cannot replace both letters without losing that information. In connected speech a close vowel may be reduced, and in certain inflected forms it is deleted: `ауыз + ы → аузы`, `орын + ы → орны`, `ерін + і → ерні`. That is a particular morphological change, not evidence that every ы or і is silent. The sounds also predate Cyrillic Kazakh: the 1929 Latin alphabet represented ы and і separately. Sources: [JIPA illustration of Kazakh](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/kazakh/353A10BD35418B48B5A6370D9F7D8CE0), [study of close-vowel deletion](https://termincom.kz/assets/pdf/311ff7626d9b5993949ccb9b41a43023.pdf), and [the 1929 alphabet](https://ortalyq.kz/1929-zhyly-abyldan-an-lipbi/).

## The support signal

```kazakh
Ы = short back pulse ●  |  І = short front pulse ●  |  ауыз + ы → аузы
```

## The limit of the rule

Do not turn shortness into silence, and do not treat the vowel as a command to strengthen a consonant. Voicing continues, the jaw opens little, and the tongue is farther back for `ы` and farther forward for `і`. Learn deletion only in verified forms: `ауыл → ауылы`, for example, keeps its vowel.

## Predict before the explanation

Sort `қыс`, `тіс`, `ыдыс`, and `іні` into back and front lanes before listening. Predict where your palm should make one short tap and where a beginner might incorrectly stretch the vowel over two counts.

## Rebuild it without the prompt

Hide the words. Place two fingers lightly on your throat: voicing should continue through the vowel. Show duration with the other hand, using a short touch for ы/і and a long stretch only for a deliberately wrong version. Rebuild four words from pictures and name their harmony lane.

## Find and repair the mistake

A learner says that ы in `қыс` has no sound and produces almost `қс`; then they overcorrect to `қыыс`. Both extremes miss the target. Restore one short voiced pulse between the consonants. Use `ауыз — аузы` to explain why the second form is genuinely different.

## Transfer to a new setting

Compare `бір бөлме`, `алты орын`, and `менің інім`. Record an intentionally overlong version and then a short version. Do not judge the voice as good or bad; listen only for extra duration and preserved meaning.

## Exercise

**Required.** Write `қыс`, `тіс`, `ыдыс`, `іні`, `ауыз`, and `орын`. Mark ы or і, the harmony lane, and one short beat in each. Then say `аузы`, `орны`, and `ерні`, and explain in writing why they do not prove that all ы and і letters are silent.

**Transfer check.** Hear two unfamiliar words containing ы or і three times and describe tongue position and duration without replacing the sound with an English or Russian model.
