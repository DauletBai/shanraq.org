## Situation and question

Two notebook entries use “strong acid” and “concentrated solution” as synonyms. One describes behaviour in water; the other describes solution composition. What evidence is needed for each claim? Recall lesson 56 on molar concentration. No tasting or mixing of samples is required.

## New terms, fully explained

**Acid strength** concerns ionisation equilibrium in a specified solvent: a strong monoprotic acid is essentially completely ionised in our dilute aqueous model; a weak acid is partly ionised. **Analytical concentration** c₀ counts the acid introduced regardless of its present form. The ionised fraction α is the share of original acid units that have ionised.

## Predict first

Predict whether a more concentrated weak acid could provide more H₃O⁺ than a very dilute strong acid. Names alone cannot decide. Compare c₀ and equilibrium information. For a weak acid, α is not a fixed fraction that remains identical at every dilution.

## Investigate or analyse given data

A supplied monoprotic HA model contains ten acid units: one appears as A⁻ with H₃O⁺, while nine remain HA. Water is omitted; this is a schematic given state, not a measured micrograph. Find α. Separate cards give c₀=0.010 mol/L with α=0.10, and c₀=0.00010 mol/L with α≈1.

| Object / step | Given / calculation | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| HA | 9 HA + A⁻ + H₃O⁺ | α = 1/10 |
| c₀ = 0.010; α = 0.10 | c₀ × α | 0.0010 mol/L |
| c₀ = 0.00010; α ≈ 1 | c₀ × α | ≈ 0.00010 mol/L |

## Worked example

The picture has α=1/10=0.10. In the first numerical card, the acid contributes approximately 0.010×0.10=0.0010 mol/L hydronium; the second gives approximately 0.00010 mol/L. With water’s contribution negligible, the partly ionised example provides more hydronium. Each card supplies its own α.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** calling weak acids harmless or assuming strong acids must be concentrated. Hazards require composition, concentration and product documentation. Do not carry α=0.10 to a different solution without evidence. HA + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + A⁻ conserves atoms and charge at any applicable extent of ionisation.

## Transfer to a new case

Transfer case: X contains a weak monoprotic acid with c₀=0.020 mol/L and supplied α=0.05. Y contains a strong acid with c₀=0.00020 mol/L. Find approximate hydronium concentrations and X/Y, assuming the dilute model applies and water contributes negligibly. What information is needed after diluting X?

## Answer and recall

**Check:** X gives 0.0010 mol/L and Y 0.00020; the ratio is five. Diluted X needs a new equilibrium state or an acid constant and calculation; its old α cannot simply be retained. Explain why strength, concentration and hazard are three distinct descriptions.

## Exercise

For c₀=0.030 mol/L and supplied α=0.020, find HA’s hydronium contribution. Does α survive every dilution unchanged?

## Answers

0.00060 mol/L under the stated assumptions; no, a new equilibrium is needed.
