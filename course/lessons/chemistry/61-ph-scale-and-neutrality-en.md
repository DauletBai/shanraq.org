## Situation and question

Two solution cards show pH 3 and pH 5. One learner says “twice as much”, another says “one hundred times”. What quantity are they comparing? Do not inspect or measure real solutions: use numerical cards and an aqueous model at 25 °C only.

## New terms, fully explained

**pH** is rigorously related to hydrogen-ion activity. For a sufficiently dilute teaching model, use pH≈−lg(c(H₃O⁺)/(1 mol/L)). The logarithm acts on a dimensionless ratio; pH is dimensionless too. At 25 °C, neutrality means c(H₃O⁺)=c(OH⁻), giving approximately pH 7 in this model.

## Predict first

Predict which has more H₃O⁺ per volume: pH 3 or pH 5. The pH numbers differ by two, but how does 10^(−pH) change? Do not infer acid strength from pH alone: composition and concentration both matter. Here compare activity, approximated by concentration.

## Investigate or analyse given data

Complete cards at 25 °C for c(H₃O⁺)=10⁻³, 10⁻⁵ and 10⁻⁷ mol/L. Find each pH. In reverse, pH 4 gives 10⁻⁴ mol/L. A one-unit step changes the concentration approximation by a factor of ten, rather than adding a fixed number of ions.

| Object / step | Given / calculation | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| 10⁻³ mol/L | −lg(10⁻³) | pH ≈ 3 |
| 10⁻⁵ mol/L | −lg(10⁻⁵) | pH ≈ 5 |
| 10⁻⁷ mol/L; 25 °C | c(H₃O⁺) = c(OH⁻) | pH ≈ 7 |

## Worked example

For pH 3 the approximation gives 10⁻³ mol/L; for pH 5 it gives 10⁻⁵. Their ratio is 10⁻³/10⁻⁵=100. The first has one hundred times the hydronium concentration in this model. The neutral point depends on temperature: equal concentrations matter more than memorising one number.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** treating 0–14 as an absolute limit for every solution, or writing “pH 7 is safe to drink”. Aqueous solutions can lie outside the familiar range; here it is just a convenient scale segment. pH does not determine contaminants, microbes or every hazard. At another temperature, neutral pH may differ from 7.

## Transfer to a new case

Transfer case: A has pH 2 and B pH 4 at the same temperature, with the approximation applicable. Find their hydronium-concentration ratio. Card C gives c(H₃O⁺)=10⁻⁹ mol/L at 25 °C. Classify it without claiming to know its complete chemical contents.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** A/B=100, with more hydronium in A; C has approximately pH 9 and is basic at 25 °C. Neither complete composition nor drinking safety follows. Recall the three reference values and explain why the temperature condition belongs in the answer.

## Exercise

For pH four and six, what is the hydronium-concentration ratio in this approximation? Is neutrality always pH seven?

## Answers

100:1; no. Neutrality means equal hydronium and hydroxide concentrations; neutral pH depends on temperature.
