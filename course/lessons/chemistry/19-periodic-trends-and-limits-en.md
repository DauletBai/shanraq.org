## Situation and question

Proton count increases along Li, Na, K. Must the atom therefore become smaller? Occupied electron shells matter alongside nuclear charge. We will compare two sets of neutral atoms. Sizes below express qualitative relationships, not measurements made with a ruler on a drawing.

## New terms, fully explained

A **periodic trend** is a recurring direction of change in a property within a specified part of the table. **Atomic radius** describes size under an agreed definition; an electron cloud has no hard boundary. Do not mix different radius definitions or compare atomic and ionic radii as though they were the same quantity. Here we compare covalent radii: half the distance between the nuclei of two identical atoms joined by a single chemical bond. The next block develops bonding; for now, keep the size definition consistent. This lesson gives no numerical radii.

## Predict first

Predict separately the size changes down Li → Na → K and across the short series Na → Mg → Al. Where is an occupied shell added? Where does shell count stay the same? Record two explanations before checking the answers.

## Investigate or analyse given data

Qualitative data for the selected covalent radii: Li is smaller than Na, and Na smaller than K; Na is larger than Mg, and Mg larger than Al. Beside these, write 2,1; 2,8,1; 2,8,8,1 and 2,8,1; 2,8,2; 2,8,3. Draw circles only to show the order of sizes. Label them “symbolic, not to scale”. Use paper rather than material samples.

## Worked example

Down group 1, additional occupied shells place outer electrons farther from the nucleus on average. Across this selected part of period 3, occupied-shell count stays constant while nuclear charge rises. Allowing for shielding, this helps explain decreasing size from Na to Mg to Al. Counting protons alone cannot explain both series.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** assuming that every property changes strictly and identically across every row. Melting point depends on a substance’s structure, not just an isolated atom’s size. Other table regions and precise values need reference data with the quantity defined. A symbol’s colour or printed size is not a measurement.

## Transfer to a new case

## Answer and recall

**Check:** Ca is generally larger than Mg and has an additional occupied shell. This does not calculate numerical melting points or arbitrary reaction rates. ## Exercise

## Answers

It increases downwards; Na > Mg > Al under a consistent radius definition; no.
