## Situation and question

Imagine the table as a map of addresses. Na and Mg are neighbours in a row, while Li and Na sit one above the other. Which pair shares a “floor” and which shares a “street”? This analogy is a starting point: the address describes patterns in atomic structure, not where a substance is found in nature.

## New terms, fully explained

A **period** is a horizontal row; a **group** is a vertical column. The long table used here numbers groups from 1 to 18. Elements follow increasing Z, not a strictly increasing average atomic mass. Our reference shows only the first 20 elements. Gaps are deliberate: moving B beneath Li to fill space would give it the wrong address. An empty position in this limited reference does not mean no element occupies it in the full table.

## Predict first

Before reading the example, predict the addresses of Na, Mg and Cl. Do Na and Cl share a period or a group? Should He belong to group 2 merely because it has two electrons? Mark which answers use an actual position in the table and which are guesses based on a single number.

## Investigate or analyse given data

Use the included first-20-elements diagram to locate H, He, Li, Ne, Na, Cl, K and Ca. Record symbol, Z, period and group for each. Trace a row first and a column second. If the diagram appears small, open the SVG itself and zoom in. Do not fill its gaps with invented symbols or slide existing entries into them.

## Worked example

Na has Z=11, period 3, group 1. Mg has Z=12, period 3, group 2. Cl has Z=17, period 3, group 17. Na and Cl therefore share a period but not a group. Li and Na share group 1 but occupy periods 2 and 3. Helium is in group 18, period 1: “group equals total electrons” is not a valid rule.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** reversing the directions, or describing group 17 as a seventh period. Older school tables may use different group labels; this course uses IUPAC numbers 1–18. Changing the table’s design does not change an element’s Z. Its position does not tell you a sample’s colour, price, availability or safety.

## Transfer to a new case

Two new cards describe element X with Z=19 and element Y with Z=20. X and Y are placeholders in this problem, not actual element symbols. Find their real symbols and addresses. Compare X with Na: what stays the same, and what differs? Check the table rather than guessing from how a name sounds.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** X is K, period 4, group 1; Y is Ca, period 4, group 2. K and Na share a group but have different periods and atomic numbers. Reconstruct the directions “period →” and “group ↓” from memory. Put He at the upper right and explain why two electrons do not move it to group 2.

## Exercise

1. Give Cl’s period and group. Where is He?
2. Do groups 3–12 exist although our extract leaves them unfilled?

## Answers

Cl: 3 and 17; He: 1 and 18; yes, in the full table.
