## Situation and question

Compare water in a closed glass with an ice cube on a plate. Their shapes differ, yet both samples consist mainly of H₂O. Did the molecules themselves change, or did their arrangement change?

## New terms, fully explained

**Particle** is a useful general word for an atom, molecule or ion. A **particle model** describes an unseen level and is tested through observable consequences. In a solid, particles generally vibrate around stable positions; in a liquid they stay near one another but can change neighbours; in a gas their separations are much larger. This is not a literal picture of water.

## Predict first

Predict whether liquid water keeps the shape of a rectangular container when poured into a round one. What about ice? Record your explanation before observing.

## Investigate or analyse given data

Pour only room-temperature water between two safe containers. Do not heat or taste anything. Observe ice without holding it for long. Without materials, use these observations: water takes its container's shape; ice retains its shape until it melts; air occupies the available space.

## Worked example

Water in a glass and on a plate remains water. In the model, its molecules move relative to their neighbours. Melting ice does not split H₂O molecules into hydrogen and oxygen atoms; their motion and arrangement change. A gas should not be drawn as a tightly packed layer at the bottom of an otherwise empty vessel.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** particles in a solid do not move at all, or a gas contains no particles. A simplified diagram does not show real paths, sizes or forces. State alone cannot reveal the substance's composition.

## Transfer to a new case

A sealed bag of air changes shape under gentle pressure. How is this different from pouring water? Name the observation separately from the model-based explanation.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** gas compresses more easily than liquid, so the bag's volume can change more readily. Draw three equal frames for solid, liquid and gas; arrange symbolic particles and label the drawings 'not to scale'. Tomorrow explain without the drawing what remains the same when ice melts.

## Knowledge check

1. Why does ice keep its shape?
2. What survives melting?

**Answers:** Particles vibrate near stable positions; H₂O molecules remain.
