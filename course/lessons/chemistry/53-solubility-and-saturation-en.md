## Situation and question

A report says some crystals remained after adding a solute. Does that necessarily establish saturation? Perhaps too little time elapsed. A conclusion needs temperature, composition and time to reach equilibrium. Analyse supplied information; do not heat substances at home.

## New terms, fully explained

**Solubility** describes an equilibrium limit for a solute in a specified solvent under stated conditions. Here S is grams of solute per 100 g of water. A **saturated solution** reaches the limit; an **unsaturated solution** is below it. Concentrated and saturated are not synonyms.

## Predict first

Predict whether stirring changes final solubility at the same temperature. Must a dark or thick-looking solution be saturated? Separate appearance, quantitative comparison with S, and process rate.

## Investigate or analyse given data

For imaginary X, S is 30 g per 100 g of water at 20 °C and 50 g per 100 g of water at 40 °C. These teaching figures describe X only. Find the limit for 50 g of water at each temperature. The denominator is water mass, not solution mass.

| Object / step | Given / calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 20 °C | S = 30 g / 100 g H₂O | 15 g / 50 g H₂O |
| 20 g X | 15 g + 5 g | 65 g + 5 g |
| 40 → 20 °C | 50 g → 30 g | 20 g crystals |

## Worked example

At 20 °C, 50 g of water dissolves up to 15 g of X. If 20 g is added and equilibrium reached, 15 g is dissolved and 5 g remains solid. Liquid solution mass is 65 g; the whole system including solid has mass 70 g. These are different weighed objects.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** treating a mixture including undissolved solid as homogeneous solution. Another is assuming heating increases every solubility. Temperature dependence varies; many gases become less soluble in water on heating at fixed partial pressure. Use data rather than a universal rule.

## Transfer to a new case

Transfer: a saturated solution at 40 °C contains 100 g of water and 50 g of X. In a calculation model it reaches equilibrium at 20 °C without water loss. How much X crystallizes? No heating or cooling experiment is assigned.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** 30 g of X remains dissolved and 20 g crystallizes. Liquid mass is 130 g, whole-system mass 150 g. Stirring changes rate but not the equilibrium limit under unchanged conditions. Reconstruct the labels: substance, solvent, temperature and basis of S.

## Exercise

At 20 °C, S=30 g per 100 g water. Add 70 g of X to 200 g water. At equilibrium how much is dissolved and solid?

## Answers

60 g dissolved and 10 g solid; liquid solution mass is 260 g.
