## Situation and question

## New terms, fully explained

A Brønsted–Lowry **acid donates a proton H⁺** and a **base accepts one**. In water, do not draw H⁺ as an isolated bare proton: use hydronium, H₃O⁺. The shorthand H⁺(aq) represents a hydrated proton. Acidity depends on the relative amounts per volume of H₃O⁺ and OH⁻, not colour or taste.

## Predict first

Consider HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻. Predict the proton donor and acceptor. Then change the example to NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻. Can water play a different role? Before reading on, mark the change in the number of H atoms in each species.

Copy both equations. Count H, O, Cl or N on each side, then add the charges. In the first equation, neutral reactants give +1 and −1 products. In the second, an equilibrium arrow allows reverse transfer; it does not mean equal amounts of every species.

## Worked example

With HCl, water accepts H⁺ and acts as a base, leaving Cl⁻. With NH₃, water donates H⁺ and acts as an acid, producing NH₄⁺ and OH⁻. A **conjugate pair** differs by one proton: HCl/Cl⁻, H₃O⁺/H₂O or NH₄⁺/NH₃. The transfer does not destroy atoms.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** requiring every base formula to contain OH. NH₃ has no such group but accepts a proton. An alkali is a water-soluble base; the school aqueous description and the proton-transfer model provide different explanatory levels. A neutral individual species does not establish that the whole solution is neutral.

## Transfer to a new case

Transfer case: HNO₃ + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + NO₃⁻. Identify the donor, acceptor and two conjugate pairs. Does the oxygen count change between H₂O and H₃O⁺? Explain why total product charge is zero although the solution contains charged species.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** HNO₃ donates and H₂O accepts; the pairs are HNO₃/NO₃⁻ and H₃O⁺/H₂O. Water and hydronium each contain one O; one proton is added. The +1 and −1 charges balance. Reconstruct both original equations and explain water’s different roles.

## Exercise

In NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻, who donates H⁺? Name NH₃’s conjugate pair.

## Answers

Water donates; NH₄⁺/NH₃. Total product charge is zero.
