## Situation and question

Water in a glass looks similar in every direction. Can that reveal the charge distribution within one molecule? Recall lesson 26: bond polarity and molecular polarity are related but distinct questions. Work with drawings and supplied information, without handling chemical samples.

## New terms, fully explained

A **polar bond** has an uneven distribution of electron density. In O–H it is shifted toward oxygen. The labels δ− on O and δ+ on H denote partial charges, not full ionic charges. H₂O is electrically neutral overall, but its bent shape gives a nonzero molecular dipole: it is **polar**.

## Predict first

Draw bent H₂O and linear CO₂. Both contain polar bonds. Predict which geometry allows the opposite contributions of identical bonds to cancel. Give a reason before reading the explanation; listing the elements alone cannot answer the question.

## Investigate or analyse given data

Draw Na⁺ and Cl⁻ on paper. Around Na⁺ orient water molecules with their oxygen ends facing inward; around Cl⁻ use their hydrogen ends. **Hydration** means interaction with surrounding water. Real molecules move; the drawing represents a simplified orientation. Colours and distances are not measurements.

| Object / step | Given / calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O | δ−: O; δ+: H | q = 0 |
| CO₂ | O=C=O | μ = 0 |
| Na⁺ / Cl⁻ | H₂O | δ− / δ+ |

## Worked example

The two bond contributions do not cancel in bent H₂O, whereas they cancel in symmetric linear CO₂. The model places δ− toward Na⁺ and δ+ toward Cl⁻. Turning a molecule does not turn water into an ion or change its formula.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** “water is positively charged”, or “a polar bond always makes a polar molecule”. A neutral molecule has zero total charge. Shape matters alongside bond polarity. Water polarity does not prove that every substance dissolves in it; interactions among all components also matter.

## Transfer to a new case

Transfer: a hypothetical linear X–Y–X molecule has two identical polar bonds with opposite contributions. Could the molecule be nonpolar? Explain without relying on colour. Change the drawing to a bent shape and reconsider cancellation.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** symmetric linear geometry allows cancellation and a nonpolar molecule despite polar bonds. Two equal nonzero contributions do not cancel in the bent model. Reconstruct “bond → shape → molecule → orientation near an ion”. Distinguish an explanatory particle model from direct observation.

## Exercise

Do polar bonds prove molecular polarity? Which end of H₂O faces Na⁺?

## Answers

No; shape matters. The oxygen end with δ− faces Na⁺. Water is neutral overall.
