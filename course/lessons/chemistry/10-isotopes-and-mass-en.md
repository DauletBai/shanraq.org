## Situation and question

Two cards both say C. One lists six protons and six neutrons; the other six protons and seven neutrons. Does the same symbol mean the same mass?

## New terms, fully explained

**Isotopes** are atoms of one element with the same proton count but different neutron counts. **Mass number** is the sum of protons and neutrons in an individual atom; do not confuse it with average atomic mass in a table. Carbon with six protons and six neutrons has mass number 12; with seven neutrons, 13. Electrons are not part of this sum. Some isotopes have unstable nuclei; radioactivity cannot be inferred from the letter C alone.

## Predict first

Predict the mass number for an atom with eight protons and ten neutrons. Another has eight protons and eight neutrons: a new element or a different isotope?

## Investigate or analyse given data

Sort paper records: P, 6p and 6n; Q, 6p and 7n; R, 8p and 8n; S, 8p and 10n. Give the atomic and mass numbers of each. This is a data exercise, not an isotope experiment; seek or handle no samples.

## Worked example

P and Q are carbon with mass numbers 12 and 13. R and S are oxygen with mass numbers 16 and 18. P and Q share six protons and therefore an element; Q and R differ in proton count and element.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** treating the average atomic mass in a periodic table as every atom's mass number. An average depends on isotope proportions in a specified sample and may not be an integer. Neutron count alone does not determine the symbol.

## Transfer to a new case

Imagine two neutral atoms each with one proton: one has no neutron, the other has one. Give their element and mass numbers. Can these counts tell how common each is in nature?

## Answer and recall

**Check:** both are hydrogen isotopes with mass numbers one and two. Abundance requires measurements of samples. Rebuild from memory: 'protons → element; protons + neutrons → mass number', then solve a fresh card.

## Knowledge check

1. Mass numbers for 6p+6n and 6p+7n?
2. Does the element change?

**Answers:** 12 and 13; no, both are carbon.
