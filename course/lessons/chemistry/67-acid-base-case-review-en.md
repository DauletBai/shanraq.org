## Situation and question

The calculation notebook says: “20 mL of 0.10 mol/L HCl and 20 mL of 0.10 mol/L NaOH gave 40 mL of water. pH seven, salt disappeared, safe to drink.” Which parts follow from a model, and which do not follow? This is a final paper exercise, not an experiment performed by the learner.

## New terms, fully explained

A report must separate **input data**, **model**, **calculation** and **conclusion**. Additional supplied conditions are final volume 0.040 L, temperature 25 °C, complete reaction without loss and an applicable ideal dilute model. Use M(NaCl)=58.5 g/mol. Purity and safety have not been supplied as conditions.

## Predict first

Predict residual reagents before arithmetic using equal concentrations, equal volumes and a 1:1 coefficient ratio. What remains of Na and Cl? Can “neutral salt solution” be replaced by “pure water”? Write your own conclusion and its limitations before reading the analysis.

## Investigate or analyse given data

Build a chain: 20 mL → 0.020 L; n(HCl) and n(NaOH); NaCl amount; NaCl mass; formal salt concentration at V=0.040 L. Separately write H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ → 2H₂O and identify spectator ions. Do not infer final volume from starting volumes alone: it is explicitly supplied.

| Object / step | Given / calculation | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| 20 mL + 20 mL; 0.10 mol/L | n(NaCl) = 0.0020 mol | m = 0.117 g |
| V = 0.040 L; 25 °C | c(NaCl) = 0.050 mol/L | pH ≈ 7 in model |
| 20 mL HCl + 18 mL NaOH | acid excess = 0.0002 mol | pH ≈ 2.28 |

## Worked example

The 0.0020 mol of each reagent is consumed. NaCl amount is 0.0020 mol, calculated mass 0.117 g and formal concentration 0.050 mol/L. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ remain in solution. Approximately pH seven follows from the chosen model at 25 °C, but no actual measurement or impurity analysis has been made.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** using pH as a measure of all solutes or permission to drink. An exact acid–base balance and safety answer different questions. An indicator with a broad transition does not establish exact mole equivalence. The ionic equation, calculation and composition claim must agree rather than replace one another.

## Transfer to a new case

Transfer case: the alkali volume was actually 18 mL at the same concentration; final volume is supplied as 0.038 L. Find excess reagent, NaCl amount and mass, and approximate pH at 25 °C. Name two safety claims that remain unsupported after correcting the calculation.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** NaOH is 0.0018 mol and HCl 0.0020 mol, leaving 0.0002 mol acid excess. NaCl is 0.0018 mol with mass 0.1053 g; c(H₃O⁺)≈0.00526 mol/L and pH≈2.28. Neither drinking nor disposal is authorised. Assess balance, units, model and conclusion limits; revisit lessons 61, 63, 64 or 66 as needed.

## Exercise

Does NaCl remain in the original 20+20 mL case? Give its mass and the conclusion’s limit.

## Answers

Yes: 0.0020 mol and 0.117 g using the supplied molar mass. Approximately pH seven is a model result at 25 °C, not proof of purity or safety.
