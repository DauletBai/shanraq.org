## Situation and question

Two classroom drawings use the same colour for their 'atoms'. Does that prove they are the same element? No: the artist chose the colour.

## New terms, fully explained

An **atom** is the smallest particle retaining an element's chemical identity. A **chemical element** groups atoms with the same number of protons in their nuclei. A **proton** has positive charge; the number of protons is the **atomic number**. One proton means hydrogen, six carbon and eight oxygen. A molecule and a simple substance are different concepts.

## Predict first

Predict: neutral atoms A and B have six and eight protons respectively. Are they the same element? What if only their neutron counts differ?

## Investigate or analyse given data

Use cards instead of chemicals: A has six protons and six neutrons; B has eight and eight; C has six and seven. Group them by element. Model spheres are not drawn to scale.

## Worked example

A and C are carbon atoms because both have six protons; B is oxygen with eight. A and C differ in neutron count without changing element identity. We will examine isotopes separately. C and O are standard chemical symbols, not arbitrary colours.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** confusing an element with one molecule or with a drawing's colour. Oxygen as an element has atomic number eight; O₂ gas is a simple substance made of two-atom molecules. Atomic number is not the atom's total mass.

## Transfer to a new case

Compare cards with one and two protons. Could dissolving table salt turn one into the other? Explain the boundary of an ordinary chemical process.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** one proton identifies hydrogen, two identify helium; dissolving does not change nuclei or atomic numbers. Rebuild the rule 'proton count → element' and test it with three cards unaided.

## Knowledge check

1. Are atoms with six and eight protons one element?
2. What fixes element identity?

**Answers:** No; proton count.
