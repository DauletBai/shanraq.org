## Situation and question

The notebook proposes: “Liquid A is acidic and B is basic, so mix them to make them safe.” What information is missing? An acid’s name or a near-neutral pH does not describe every hazard. Use fictional paper labels only; do not open or investigate real containers.

## New terms, fully explained

A **hazard** is a capacity to cause harm; **risk** also depends on exposure and circumstances. Labels and safety data sheets describe a product and its handling. pH is only one property: it cannot replace information about toxicity, oxidising properties, compatibility or concentration. No colour or smell does not mean no hazard.

## Predict first

Predict which statement helps a decision: “A: pH 2” or “A: do not mix with other products; read manufacturer instructions”. Neither authorises an experiment. List missing information before the answer: exact composition, concentrations, conditions and possible interaction products.

## Investigate or analyse given data

Fictional cards read: A—cleaning product, pH 2, “do not mix”; B—another product, pH 11, “use separately”; C—unknown liquid without a label. Choose actions: do not mix, do not use the unknown substance, and contact a responsible adult. Calculate no mixing proportions and assign no experiment.

## Worked example

A and B retain their explicit mixing prohibitions. For C, unknown composition is itself reason to stop investigating and tell a responsible adult. A pure-reagent neutralisation equation cannot describe a multicomponent cleaner. Hazardous products or heat may result even if final acidity decreases.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** turning a calculation into instructions for treating a spill, skin or eyes, or trying to “correct” a hazardous mixture with another product. In a real situation follow manufacturer instructions and guidance from a responsible adult or emergency services. This lesson supplies no incident-cleanup procedure and does not replace specialist training.

## Transfer to a new case

Transfer case: an online message says “After adding the product, pH became seven, so the liquid may now be poured away or drunk”. Which two conclusions are unsupported? Choose a safe response for the paper scenario. Do not seek extra evidence by touching or smelling a liquid.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** pH seven establishes neither drinking safety nor disposal permission. An unknown mixture remains outside a learner’s permitted handling; tell a responsible adult and use official instructions. Reconstruct “identify → documentation → prohibitions → safe decision”, distinguishing chemical neutrality from safety.

## Exercise

Two product cards show pH two and eleven. May you calculate household mixing proportions?

## Answers

No. Composition and compatibility are unknown: do not mix; follow labels and contact a responsible adult.
