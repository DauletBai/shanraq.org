## Situation and question

The notebook claims: “The acid and alkali disappeared, leaving pure water.” Where did Na and Cl go? Compare the solution formula with its particle contents. Consider a calculation model of strong monoprotic HCl reacting with aqueous NaOH only; no experiment is assigned.

## New terms, fully explained

In this case, **neutralisation** consumes H₃O⁺ and OH⁻ to form water. **Spectator ions** remain in the same form before and after reaction. They cancel from an equation, not from the solution’s contents. Complete neutralisation does not give pH 7 for every acid and base combination.

## Predict first

Predict the products of HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l). Which species remain in solution? If Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are omitted from the net equation, have their atoms vanished? Answer before reading on. NaCl(aq) does not represent separate salt molecules in this model.

## Investigate or analyse given data

With water represented explicitly, write H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻ + Na⁺ + OH⁻ → Na⁺ + Cl⁻ + 2H₂O. Cancel identical spectator ions. Count H and O in the remaining equation, then total charges. Two waters are appropriate for the hydronium representation, rather than the shorthand H⁺ equation.

| Object / step | Given / calculation | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ | → | 2H₂O |
| Na⁺, Cl⁻ | before and after | spectators |
| K⁺, NO₃⁻ | before and after | spectators |

## Worked example

The net equation H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ → 2H₂O has four H and two O on each side, with total charge zero on both. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ remain hydrated. Exact equivalent amounts of strong acid and alkali give approximately pH 7 in an ideal dilute model at 25 °C—but a salt solution, not pure water.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** saying “all ions were neutralised”, or transferring pH≈7 to salts of weak acids and bases. More generally, acid–base equilibria can alter pH. An indicator colour does not demonstrate the disappearance of all solutes. This theory is not a method for making unknown household mixtures safe.

## Transfer to a new case

Transfer case: HNO₃(aq) + KOH(aq) → KNO₃(aq) + H₂O(l). Identify the spectators and reconstruct the hydronium net equation. Explain why a reading near pH 7 under the model’s conditions still does not make the product distilled water.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** K⁺ and NO₃⁻ are spectators; the net equation is again H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ → 2H₂O. Salt ions remain. Neutrality concerns acid–base state, whereas purity concerns composition. Reconstruct all three representations, checking atoms and charge separately.

## Exercise

Identify the spectator ions for HCl and NaOH. Do they disappear from solution?

## Answers

Na⁺ and Cl⁻; no. H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ → 2H₂O does not remove them from the contents.
