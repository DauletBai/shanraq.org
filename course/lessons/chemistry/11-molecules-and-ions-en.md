## Situation and question

Water is written H₂O and salt NaCl. Do both formulas mean the same kind of separate 'atom packets'?

## New terms, fully explained

A **molecule** is an electrically neutral group of bonded atoms that can be considered a distinct particle; water molecules contain two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. An **ion** carries charge. Solid sodium chloride is an ordered lattice of positive sodium ions, Na⁺, and negative chloride ions, Cl⁻. NaCl gives their 1:1 ratio as a formula unit of the crystal; it is not a separate salt molecule. Dissolved salt is represented by ions dispersed in water.

## Predict first

Predict whether a single Na⁺ can be a neutral molecule. What do the subscript ₂ in H₂O and the missing subscripts in NaCl signify?

## Investigate or analyse given data

Use paper circles to build separate groups of two H and one O; then make a repeating pattern alternating Na⁺ and Cl⁻. Do not label a single square in the pattern a 'real molecule'. These are symbolic, unscaled models, not explanations of every bonding force.

## Worked example

Three H₂O groups stand for three water molecules, six H and three O atoms in all. Three NaCl formula units correspond to three Na⁺ and three Cl⁻ in the lattice. Dissolving the crystal distributes ions in water; NaCl does not turn into H₂O.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** calling every substance molecular and drawing isolated NaCl pairs inside its crystal. Other substances have atomic or metallic structures; we will return to them later. Ball models do not reproduce electron-cloud shapes.

## Transfer to a new case

Sugar and salt can both disappear from view in water. Does transparency prove they produce the same kind of dissolved particles? What composition evidence would help?

## Answer and recall

**Check:** transparency does not identify particles; evidence about the substances and a measurement method is needed. From memory label 'H₂O: distinct molecule' and 'NaCl: ion ratio in a crystal'.

## Knowledge check

1. Is there a separate NaCl molecule in an ordinary crystal?
2. What does H₂O mean?

**Answers:** No, NaCl is an ion ratio; a water molecule has two H and one O.
