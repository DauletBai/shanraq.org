## Situation and question

Water is added to a solution. Salt amount remains the same but concentration changes. Which quantity is conserved? Compare dilution to a specified final volume with mixing solutions by mass. Work on paper only.

## New terms, fully explained

**Dilution** adds solvent. If solute is neither lost nor reacting, n₁=n₂. Since n=cV, c₁V₁=c₂V₂. V₂ is final solution volume. For mass fractions, solute mass is conserved under the same restrictions: w₁m₁=w₂m₂.

## Predict first

Predict whether c increases on adding pure water. Must final volume equal the exact sum of starting solution and added water volumes? Concentration uses a solute balance; exact volume needs measurement or an explicit assumption.

## Investigate or analyse given data

Start with 100 mL at c₁=0.50 mol/L and final V₂=250 mL. Convert volumes to litres and find n before and after. Separately consider mixing 100 g of 10% and 100 g of 4% solutions of the same solute without reaction or losses.

| Object / step | Given / calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 100 → 250 mL | 0.50 × 100 / 250 | 0.20 mol/L |
| 100 g; 10% + 100 g; 4% | 14 / 200 | 7% |
| 50 g; 10% + 150 g; 4% | 11 / 200 | 5.5% |

## Worked example

In the first case n=0.50×0.100=0.050 mol, and c₂=0.050/0.250=0.20 mol/L. In the second, solute mass is 10+4=14 g in 200 g total, so w=0.07=7%. Averaging percentages works here because solution masses are equal.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** averaging percentages for any masses, or treating V₂ as added water volume. Mixing 50 g at 10% with 150 g at 4% gives solute masses 5 and 6 g, hence 11/200=5.5%, not 7%. Balance first, divide second.

## Transfer to a new case

Transfer: dilute 40 mL at 0.75 mol/L to a final 120 mL. Find n and c₂. Separately add 40 g of water to 80 g of a 15% solution. Find the final mass fraction without losses.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** n=0.030 mol and c₂=0.25 mol/L. The mass case gives 12 g of solute in 120 g of solution, or 10%. Do not apply dilution equations to reaction or precipitation without a new balance. Recall: same component, no losses, correct final volume or mass.

## Exercise

Add 100 g of water to 50 g of a 12% solution. Find mass fraction and the conserved quantity.

## Answers

6 g of component in 150 g gives 4%; component mass is conserved without losses or reaction.
