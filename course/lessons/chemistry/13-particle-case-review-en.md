## Situation and question

Return to our first question: salt crystals are no longer visible after dissolving. Which claims can we now justify, and what remains untested?

## New terms, fully explained

Keep **three levels of description** separate: observation (grains disappear from view), model (Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions spread among water molecules), and chemical notation (H₂O and NaCl). A model is not a photograph. **Conservation of matter** in dissolving means atoms do not appear from nowhere or vanish; in an actual investigation, mass is checked by weighing a closed vessel. An open vessel can exchange matter with its surroundings.

## Predict first

Predict the scale reading before and after five grams of salt fully dissolve in 100 grams of water inside a closed vessel, if the empty vessel weighs 50 grams. What exactly is weighed?

## Investigate or analyse given data

Data: empty vessel, 50 g; water, 100 g; salt, 5 g. The vessel stays closed, with no spill or addition. Make a table for 'before', 'after', 'observation' and 'model'. This is a calculation; no tasting or hands-on experiment is required.

## Worked example

Before, the total is 155 g; after, 155 g is expected because the scale includes vessel and all contents. Clarity alone does not prove that reading. In the model, ions formerly in a crystal spread out, while water molecules remain water. Ordinary paper filtration will not separate dissolved ions from water.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** 'the crystal vanished, so mass fell' or 'separate NaCl molecules float in the solution'. Do not infer an unknown solution's composition from a calculated mass alone. Measurement precision and a closed vessel are conditions of the test.

## Transfer to a new case

New case: 80 g of water and 10 g of sugar in a closed vessel weighing 40 g. Give the expected total, distinguish observation from model, and explain why sugar particles cannot simply be replaced with Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** expect 130 g. Sugar and salt particles have different compositions; transparency does not identify them. From memory rebuild lessons 6–13: state → motion → element → charge → isotope → molecule/ion → formula → model test. Repeat the case after a week.

## Knowledge check

1. Closed-system mass of 50+100+5 g?
2. Does clarity establish composition?

**Answers:** 155 g; no.
