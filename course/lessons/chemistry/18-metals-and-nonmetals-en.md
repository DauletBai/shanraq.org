## Situation and question

You have three rows of reference data, not unknown samples. Aluminium conducts electricity and can undergo plastic deformation. Graphite conducts electricity but is brittle. Sulfur is brittle and conducts poorly. Is the single property “conducts” enough to classify a substance as a metal? Write a testable rule before checking it.

## New terms, fully explained

Here a **metal** is a class of simple substances with a characteristic combination of properties, such as conductivity and plasticity. **Plasticity** means changing shape without breaking under suitable conditions. **Brittleness** is a tendency to fracture during deformation. A **nonmetal** is not necessarily a gas. Properties concern a substance and its structure, not the colour of an element’s table square.

## Worked example

Both aluminium properties fit metallic behaviour. Graphite combines conductivity with brittleness; it is a known form of carbon, a nonmetallic element. One property alone therefore fails as a reliable classification rule. Sulfur gives another combination. Diamond is also carbon but differs from graphite in structure and electrical properties.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** identifying every shiny object as metal or every nonmetal as an insulator without exceptions. Many materials shine, and objects can be coated or made of mixtures. Table position provides guidance, but a photograph cannot establish that an unknown household object is a pure simple substance.
