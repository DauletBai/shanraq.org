## Situation and question

In a video, a drop of food colouring gradually colours still water. The colour changes even without a spoon. Does water somehow 'stir itself'?

## New terms, fully explained

**Diffusion** is spreading caused by random particle motion; in liquids it is usually slower than transport by bulk flow. **Concentration** describes how much of a selected substance is in a given volume of mixture. On average, particles spread from higher to lower concentration, although individual particles move in every direction. A visible colour boundary is not a boundary between individual molecules.

## Predict first

Predict whether colour will be stronger near the drop or at the opposite wall one minute later. How could flow created while pouring change the answer?

## Investigate or analyse given data

Analyse these model data only: just after addition, the bright region fills 1/10 of the glass; after ten minutes, 6/10; after an hour, colour is nearly uniform. This is not an instruction to use dye. No individual molecules were measured, and the water may not have been perfectly still.

## Worked example

Observation: the visible coloured region expanded. Explanation: dye particles spread as they move, and possible water currents speed up the process. To isolate diffusion, compare matched containers, temperature and drop placement while controlling liquid flow.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** every particle heads deliberately from a 'crowded' region to an 'empty' one. Motion is random; net spreading is statistical. Colour does not prove that a solution is safe or perfectly uniform at molecular scale.

## Transfer to a new case

Two gases mix over time in a closed vessel. How is this like dye in water, and how may their rates differ? Do not smell unknown substances or open vessels for this activity.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** particle motion explains both, and spreading is generally faster in gases. Make two notebook columns, 'seen' and 'model'. State which extra data could distinguish diffusion from mixing by flow.

## Knowledge check

1. What is visible in the dye case?
2. Does every particle move one way?

**Answers:** The coloured region expands; no, individual motion is random.
