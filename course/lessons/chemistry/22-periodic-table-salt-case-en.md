## Situation and question

Return to the clear liquid from block 1. The case confirms that pure NaCl was originally dissolved. Explain its composition through elements and ions. The table helps check notation but cannot identify every clear liquid. Do not taste the sample or start new experiments: use only the case data.

## New terms, fully explained

**Evidence** here means data supporting a particular conclusion. An **assumption** still needs checking. An **ion ratio** compares counts of different kinds of particles. NaCl describes a one-to-one ratio of Na⁺ to Cl⁻ in an ordinary crystal. It is not a picture of a separate two-atom molecule or a promise of metallic sodium’s properties.

## Predict first

Predict whether a model containing only two Na⁺ and one Cl⁻ can represent an electrically neutral formula portion of ordinary NaCl. Write the charge sum. Then decide whether a colourless solution independently confirms NaCl. State which evidence supports each answer.

## Investigate or analyse given data

Build a four-row paper dossier. Na: Z=11, period 3, group 1, neutral arrangement 2,8,1. Cl: Z=17, period 3, group 17, arrangement 2,8,7. Na⁺: 11p, 10e. Cl⁻: 17p, 18e. Add columns “supported conclusion” and “not yet established”. Represent particles with symbolic cards only.

## Worked example

The charges are 11−10=+1 and 17−18=−1. Equal ion counts give zero total: four Na⁺ and four Cl⁻ give +4−4=0. Two Na⁺ and one Cl⁻ give +1, so that set is not a neutral NaCl formula portion. Card sizes and colours do not represent actual ion sizes or colours.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** declaring an unknown liquid salty just because Na and Cl can be found in the table. Composition was supplied in the case; without it, suitable independent evidence is needed. Do not derive neutron counts from 35.45 or move Na⁺ into period 2. A compound and the simple substances of its elements have different properties.

## Transfer to a new case

Another set contains Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻. How many singly charged anions are required per cation for zero total charge? Calculate first, then propose a ratio notation. This is a balance exercise, not a claim that neutrality alone proves the existence of any invented substance.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** one Mg²⁺ and two Cl⁻ give +2−2=0; the 1:2 ratio corresponds to MgCl₂. Explain elements, positions, neutral atoms, ions, balance and limits in that order. The next block will develop bonds and formulas further. Keep the initial answer, correction and reason for change: the notebook should show the reasoning process rather than only a correct result.

## Exercise

1. Find the total charge of 2Na⁺ and Cl⁻. What ratio does NaCl need?
2. How many Cl⁻ balance Mg²⁺?
3. Does clarity establish composition?

## Answers

+1; 1:1; two; no. Explain each using evidence and the limit of the conclusion.
