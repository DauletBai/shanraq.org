# How do we find component masses from a target fraction?

_Лид (summary):_ **Plan solution composition on paper and verify it by working backward.**

Use paper, a calculator and supplied data only. Do not heat, taste or smell samples, mix household chemicals or perform electrical experiments. Clarity and ordinary filtration do not establish drinking safety.

## Situation and question

Describe 250 g of sugar solution containing 8% sugar by mass. This is a composition problem, not a home laboratory recipe. The denominator is total solution mass. Which two component masses must add to 250 g?

## New terms, fully explained

From w=m(solute)/m(solution), obtain m(solute)=w×m(solution). In a two-component system, water mass equals solution mass minus solute mass. A **reverse check** substitutes the masses into the original definition, detecting an incorrect denominator or percentage conversion.

## Predict first

Predict whether an 8% solute mass can exceed half the solution mass. Compare plans for 100 g and 250 g of the same composition. Does multiplying both component masses by 2.5 preserve the fraction?

## Investigate or analyse given data

Write the target 250 g and w=0.08. Build a solute–water–total table. Assume pure components, complete dissolution and no losses. Without these conditions the composition can change; arithmetic does not establish actual preparation accuracy.

| Object / step | Given / calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 250 g; 8% | 0.08 × 250 | 20 g + 230 g |
| 400 g; 5% | 0.05 × 400 | 20 g + 380 g |
| 8 g; 5% | 8 / 0.05 | 160 g |

## Worked example

Sugar mass is 0.08×250=20 g and water mass 250−20=230 g. Check 20+230=250 g and 20/250=0.08. Combining 20 g of sugar with 250 g of water instead gives 270 g at about 7.41%, not 8%.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** treating “250 g of solution” as “250 g of solvent”. For an impure reagent, total reagent mass cannot silently replace pure solute mass. Here purity is supplied; impurity problems need another balance and do not instruct learners to handle unknown materials.

## Transfer to a new case

Transfer: plan 400 g of a 5% sugar solution on paper. Find both masses, check their sum and ratio, then find the solution mass at the same fraction containing 8 g of sugar. Include units.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** 20 g of sugar and 380 g of water; 20/400=0.05. With 8 g of sugar, solution mass is 8/0.05=160 g, containing 152 g of water. Equal sugar masses can belong to different compositions; specify the fraction and total mass.

## Exercise

Find component masses for 300 g of 4% sugar solution and check.

## Answers

12 g of sugar and 288 g of water; 12/300=0.04.

## When to revisit

Close the page and, after 10 minutes, reconstruct the map, define the terms in your own words and solve the new case. Repeat without hints after a day and a week. If an error persists, return to the worked example and write down the missing step.
