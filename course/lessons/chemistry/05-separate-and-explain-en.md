## Situation and question

Imagine a mixture of clean sand, table salt and room-temperature water. The task is to separate sand and obtain clearer salty water, then explain how salt might remain without heating anything at home.

## New terms, fully explained

**Separating a mixture** uses different properties of its components; it does not turn one substance into another. **Settling** is the collection of larger insoluble particles at the bottom under gravity. **Filtration** lets liquid pass through a porous barrier while sufficiently large solid pieces stay behind. The liquid that passes is the **filtrate**. **Evaporation** is water changing to gas; as water slowly evaporates from salty water, salt can remain. This does not prove the remaining salt is pure: other dissolved substances may remain too.

A **pore** is a tiny hole in a filter. Paper is **porous** because liquid can pass between its fibres. Particle and pore size determine what is retained; dissolved salt usually passes through ordinary filter paper. **Microorganisms** are tiny living things unseen without special equipment; clear filtrate is not thereby safe to drink.

## Predict first

Plan a route before reading the answer. Choose A, filter first and then consider evaporation, or B, evaporate the sandy mixture first. Where does the sand end up in each case? Where is dissolved salt after filtration?

## Investigate or analyse given data

With an adult's permission, mix clean dry sand, a little table salt and room-temperature water. Let it settle; carefully pour through a paper filter into another glass. Do not heat anything or taste the filtrate. Record what remains on paper and what passes. Without materials, use these given observations: visible sand grains remain on the paper; a clear liquid passes through.

## Worked example

Worked route: sand does not dissolve and is retained by the filter; water with dissolved salt passes into the filtrate. Label the receiving glass 'water + dissolved salt', never 'pure water'. In principle, water in an open vessel can evaporate at room temperature over a long period and leave salt behind. That is an explanation, not an at-home assignment or a guarantee of purity.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** 'The filter removed everything.' It retains particles larger than its effective pores; dissolved substances and microorganisms may pass. This experiment never establishes water as safe to drink. Residue left by evaporation need not be pure salt either.

## Transfer to a new case

New mixture: rice, sugar and water. What can a sieve retain, what stays dissolved, and what passes through ordinary filter paper? Explain why a sieve and a filter address different scales.

## Answer and recall

**Check:** a sieve can retain large rice grains; dissolved sugar passes through ordinary paper with the water. Write a method-choice rule: which property differs → which method exploits it → what remains unknown.
