## Situation and question

How can an atom as a whole be neutral when it contains positively charged particles? We must account for negative charge too.

## New terms, fully explained

The **nucleus** is the tiny central region containing protons and neutrons; it holds almost all of an atom's mass. A **neutron** has no electric charge. An **electron** has negative charge and occupies a region around the nucleus. A neutral atom has equal numbers of electrons and protons. An **ion** forms when an atom or group of atoms loses or gains electrons. Drawn circles are a classroom aid, not planetary electron paths.

## Predict first

Predict the charge of a particle with three protons and two electrons. What if it has four electrons? Do neutrons affect the sign?

## Investigate or analyse given data

Use paper cards labelled '+', '0' and '−'; connect no electrical equipment. Case A has three protons, four neutrons and three electrons. Case B has the same nucleus and two electrons. Case C has the same nucleus and four electrons. Add charges only, treating their unit magnitudes as equal.

## Worked example

A is neutral: +3 and −3 sum to zero. B has charge +1 and C has charge −1. All three are the same element, atomic number three, because proton count stays fixed. Cards count charge correctly but do not locate electrons precisely.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** including neutrons in a charge sum or claiming loss of an electron creates a new element. The nucleus in classroom diagrams is vastly oversized compared with the atom; separations and electron clouds are simplified.

## Transfer to a new case

A particle has eight protons, eight neutrons and ten electrons. What element and formal charge does it have? Do these numbers prove it exists as a free ion in a glass of water?

## Answer and recall

**Check:** it is oxygen with formal charge −2. The count alone cannot establish its stability or environment. Close the page and explain why protons define the element while the proton–electron difference defines charge.

## Knowledge check

1. How many electrons in a neutral atom with three protons?
2. Charge with two electrons?

**Answers:** Three; +1.
