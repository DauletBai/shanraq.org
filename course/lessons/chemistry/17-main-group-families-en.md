## Situation and question

The notebook contains a hypothesis: because Na is above K, their simple substances must be identical. What actually unites members of a group? Use element cards rather than samples of metals or gases. Similar structure supports a search for patterns; it does not remove every difference between substances.

## New terms, fully explained

An **element family** is a name for a group with shared chemical features. Li, Na and K are alkali metals in group 1. H is in that group but is not an alkali metal. Mg and Ca are alkaline earth metals in group 2. F and Cl are halogens in group 17. He, Ne and Ar are noble gases in group 18.

## Predict first

Predict which pair has more similar outer-shell structure in its neutral atoms: Li and Na, or Na and Cl. Write a reason before checking. Separately decide whether H being above Li makes hydrogen a metal. A question about atomic structure is different from a question about a substance’s appearance.

## Investigate or analyse given data

Make a paper table: element, group, electron arrangement, outer-shell population. Data: Li 2,1; Na 2,8,1; K 2,8,8,1; Mg 2,8,2; Ca 2,8,8,2; F 2,7; Cl 2,8,7; He 2; Ne 2,8; Ar 2,8,8. Find repeated final numbers before comparing their group positions.

## Worked example

Li and Na each have one outer electron but different numbers of occupied shells. F and Cl each have seven outer electrons. Ne and Ar have eight; He has a filled first and only shell containing two. This is why requiring eight outer electrons of every group 18 element would fail for helium.

## A common mistake

**Mistake:** treating similarity as a promise of identical reaction rates, melting points or safety. Properties vary within a family. “Noble gas” does not mean “never forms compounds”. Do not test alkali metals with water or make halogens. All work in this lesson uses supplied data.
