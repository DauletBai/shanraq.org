# Negative numbers: distance also extends left of zero

_Lead (summary):_ **We understand negative numbers through debt, temperature, and direction, move on a number line, and separate a number’s sign from an operation sign.**

## Where we are on the map

We extend the number line from natural numbers to integers.

![Seven steps to the right from −3 ending at 4](/static/course/mathematics/map-15-negative-numbers-en.svg)

## Begin with a familiar image

A temperature three degrees below `0°C` is `−3°C`. If it rises by seven degrees, we move seven steps to the right on the number line.

## Precise meaning

A negative number represents a value below zero. A minus attached to a number is its sign; a minus between two numbers can mean subtraction.

## The lesson's support signal

`meaning → model → calculation → check → explain in your own words`

right → increase; left → decrease; opposite numbers are equally far from zero

## Worked example

`−3 + 7 = 4`: seven steps right from −3 pass −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, and end at 4.

## Example with a fading prompt

Change the numbers in the example and repeat the solution without copying its steps. Estimate first, calculate exactly, and check against the original condition.

## Recall without a prompt

1. Define the main idea in one sentence.
2. Rebuild the support signal from memory.
3. Solve the example with different numbers and explain why every step is valid.

## Find and correct the mistake

Saying `−5` is greater than `−2` is wrong. −5 lies farther left on the number line, so `−5 < −2`.

## Transfer to a new setting

Apply the same relationship to something you can measure yourself. Name the quantities, units, and the boundary within which the model is valid. Check the answer by a second representation or an inverse operation.

## Exercise

**Required.** Show `−6 + 9`, `4 − 11`, and `−3 − 5` on a number line and describe the direction of every movement.

**With your own data.** Create one everyday example with the same structure and show it in words, a formula, and a check.

**Optional.** Seven days later, change the numbers and repeat without the support map.

## Open perspective

Divisibility reveals how numbers are built from factors and prepares us to simplify fractions.
