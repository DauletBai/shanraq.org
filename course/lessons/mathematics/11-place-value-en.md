# Place value: a digit’s position shows the size of its unit

_Lead (summary):_ **We bundle units into tens, hundreds, and thousands, read zero as an empty place, and expand numbers so written operations make sense.**

## Where we are on the map

We move from counting objects to the structure of multidigit numbers.

![Thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones in the number 4,072](/static/course/mathematics/map-11-place-value-en.svg)

## Begin with a familiar image

Just as ten small currency units can be exchanged for one larger unit, ten units in one place are bundled into one unit of the next place.

## Precise meaning

Place value is a digit’s position in a numeral. From right to left we have ones, tens, hundreds, and thousands; zero preserves a place that contains no units.

## The lesson's support signal

`meaning → model → calculation → check → explain in your own words`

10 ones = 1 ten → 10 tens = 1 hundred → 10 hundreds = 1 thousand

## Worked example

`4,072 = 4 × 1000 + 0 × 100 + 7 × 10 + 2`. We read it as four thousand seventy-two.

## Example with a fading prompt

Change the numbers in the example and repeat the solution without copying its steps. Estimate first, calculate exactly, and check against the original condition.

## Recall without a prompt

1. Define the main idea in one sentence.
2. Rebuild the support signal from memory.
3. Solve the example with different numbers and explain why every step is valid.

## Find and correct the mistake

Writing `3,040 = 3 × 1000 + 4 × 100` is wrong: 4 occupies the tens place, so its contribution is `4 × 10`.

## Transfer to a new setting

Apply the same relationship to something you can measure yourself. Name the quantities, units, and the boundary within which the model is valid. Check the answer by a second representation or an inverse operation.

## Exercise

**Required.** Expand `6,205`, `980`, and `10,040`, name the value of every digit, and round each number to the nearest hundred.

**With your own data.** Create one everyday example with the same structure and show it in words, a formula, and a check.

**Optional.** Seven days later, change the numbers and repeat without the support map.

## Open perspective

Place value makes regrouping in addition and subtraction understandable.
