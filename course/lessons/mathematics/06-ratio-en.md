# Ratio: a recipe that survives every scale

_Lead (summary):_ **We read `2:3` as a relationship between two quantities, build tables of equivalent ratios, and find a unit rate.**

## Where we are on the map

A fraction measures one quantity; a ratio compares two quantities in a stated order.

![Scaling the ratio 2:3 by two to obtain 4:6](/static/course/mathematics/map-06-ratio-en.svg)

## Begin with a familiar image

If a drink uses two parts juice to three parts water, doubling the recipe must change both amounts together.

## Precise meaning

Order matters in `a:b`. After matching units, multiplying or dividing both terms by the same factor preserves the ratio.

## The lesson's support signal

`meaning → model → calculation → check → explain in your own words`

state the order → align units → find one part → scale both quantities together

## Worked example

`2:3 = 4:6` because both terms were multiplied by 2. Cross-products confirm it: `2 × 6 = 3 × 4`.

## Example with a fading prompt

Change the numbers in the example and repeat the solution without copying its steps. Estimate first, calculate exactly, and check against the original condition.

## Recall without a prompt

1. Define the main idea in one sentence.
2. Rebuild the support signal from memory.
3. Solve the example with different numbers and explain why every step is valid.

## Find and correct the mistake

Changing only the first term from `2:3` to `4:3` does not preserve the recipe; both quantities need the same scale factor.

## Transfer to a new setting

Apply the same relationship to something you can measure yourself. Name the quantities, units, and the boundary within which the model is valid. Check the answer by a second representation or an inverse operation.

## Exercise

**Required.** Blue and white paint are mixed in a `3:5` ratio. Find each amount in 32 litres of mixture.

**With your own data.** Create one everyday example with the same structure and show it in words, a formula, and a check.

**Optional.** Seven days later, change the numbers and repeat without the support map.

## Open perspective

Percent is a widely used ratio language built around one hundred parts.
