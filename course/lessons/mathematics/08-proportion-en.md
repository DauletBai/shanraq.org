# Proportion and scale: which relationship must stay fixed?

_Lead (summary):_ **We solve proportions through an invariant ratio, distinguish direct and inverse relationships, and check units.**

## Where we are on the map

We move from equivalent ratios to finding an unknown quantity.

![The equality 2/3 = 6/9 produced by scaling both quantities by three](/static/course/mathematics/map-08-proportion-en.svg)

## Begin with a familiar image

If 2 cm on a map represents 3 km, then 6 cm at the same scale represents 9 km.

## Precise meaning

In direct proportion both quantities change by the same factor; in inverse proportion their product remains constant.

## The lesson's support signal

`meaning → model → calculation → check → explain in your own words`

what stays fixed? → direct or inverse? → units → calculate → check meaning

## Worked example

`2/3 = 6/9` because both values tripled; `2 × 9 = 3 × 6` provides a check.

## Example with a fading prompt

Change the numbers in the example and repeat the solution without copying its steps. Estimate first, calculate exactly, and check against the original condition.

## Recall without a prompt

1. Define the main idea in one sentence.
2. Rebuild the support signal from memory.
3. Solve the example with different numbers and explain why every step is valid.

## Find and correct the mistake

Saying twice as many equally productive workers need twice as much time is wrong: workers × time stays constant.

## Transfer to a new setting

Apply the same relationship to something you can measure yourself. Name the quantities, units, and the boundary within which the model is valid. Check the answer by a second representation or an inverse operation.

## Exercise

**Required.** Three litres of concentrate mix with 12 litres of water. Find each component in 35 litres of the same solution.

**With your own data.** Create one everyday example with the same structure and show it in words, a formula, and a check.

**Optional.** Seven days later, change the numbers and repeat without the support map.

## Open perspective

The checkpoint combines fractions, ratio, percent, and proportion in unfamiliar settings.
