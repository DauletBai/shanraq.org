# Percent: one hundredth instead of three formulas

_Lead (summary):_ **We connect percent to fractions and decimals, calculate discounts and growth through a unit, and identify the base from which a percentage is taken.**

## Where we are on the map

We study a special ratio: comparison with one hundred.

![Thirty-seven shaded cells out of one hundred showing 37% = 37/100](/static/course/mathematics/map-07-percent-en.svg)

## Begin with a familiar image

If 37 of 100 equal cells are shaded, the amount is `37/100 = 0.37 = 37%`.

## Precise meaning

Percent means one hundredth. Every problem needs a named 100% base; when the base changes, the same amount represents a different percentage.

## The lesson's support signal

`meaning → model → calculation → check → explain in your own words`

name the 100% base → convert percent to a fraction → multiply → estimate the size

## Worked example

15% of 80,000 tenge is `80,000 × 0.15 = 12,000`; the discounted price is `68,000`.

## Example with a fading prompt

Change the numbers in the example and repeat the solution without copying its steps. Estimate first, calculate exactly, and check against the original condition.

## Recall without a prompt

1. Define the main idea in one sentence.
2. Rebuild the support signal from memory.
3. Solve the example with different numbers and explain why every step is valid.

## Find and correct the mistake

A 20% decrease followed by a 20% increase does not restore the original value because the second percentage uses a smaller new base.

## Transfer to a new setting

Apply the same relationship to something you can measure yourself. Name the quantities, units, and the boundary within which the model is valid. Check the answer by a second representation or an inverse operation.

## Exercise

**Required.** Apply a 15% discount to 80,000 tenge, then 10% to the new price, and find the total percentage decrease.

**With your own data.** Create one everyday example with the same structure and show it in words, a formula, and a check.

**Optional.** Seven days later, change the numbers and repeat without the support map.

## Open perspective

Proportion makes the invariant relationship in percent and scale problems explicit.
