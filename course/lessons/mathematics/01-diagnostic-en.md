# Entry diagnosis: find the first missing support

_Lead (summary):_ **Ten short problems locate the right starting point without judging the learner or demanding perfect recall of school rules.**

## Where we are on the map

Diagnosis is the gateway to the course. It evaluates the learning chain, rather than the person, and finds its first weak link.

![Diagnostic route leading to numbers, fractions, ratios, percentages, and equations](/static/course/mathematics/map-01-diagnostic-en.svg)

## Begin with a familiar image

A doctor checks symptoms before choosing a treatment. Here you mark every answer with `+`, `?`, or `−`, so we observe confidence and reasoning as well as the final number.

## Precise meaning

A `+` means you can explain the method; `?` means you have an answer but doubt the reason; `−` means you do not know how to begin. The first `?` or `−` identifies the starting point.

## The lesson's support signal

`meaning → model → calculation → check → explain in your own words`

problem → my method → answer → explanation → confidence mark

## Worked example

Dividing 12 objects into four equal groups gives 3 in each group. `12 ÷ 4 = 3`; the explanation names what the division means.

## Example with a fading prompt

Change the numbers in the example and repeat the solution without copying its steps. Estimate first, calculate exactly, and check against the original condition.

## Recall without a prompt

1. Define the main idea in one sentence.
2. Rebuild the support signal from memory.
3. Solve the example with different numbers and explain why every step is valid.

## Find and correct the mistake

Calling the 8 in `3/8` “eight shaded pieces” is wrong. Eight says how many equal parts make the whole; three says how many of them are selected.

## Transfer to a new setting

Apply the same relationship to something you can measure yourself. Name the quantities, units, and the boundary within which the model is valid. Check the answer by a second representation or an inverse operation.

## Exercise

**Required.** Answer all ten problems and mark each one with `+`, `?`, or `−`. Explain your first uncertain answer in two sentences.

**With your own data.** Create one everyday example with the same structure and show it in words, a formula, and a check.

**Optional.** Seven days later, change the numbers and repeat without the support map.

## Open perspective

Your result sends you to the earliest support you need: number meaning, fractions, ratio, percent, or equations.
