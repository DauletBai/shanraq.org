# Order of operations and estimation: predict before calculating

_Lead (summary):_ **We read the structure of parentheses, perform operations in the correct order, and use an advance estimate to catch unreasonable results.**

## Where we are on the map

The four basic operations now meet in one expression.

![Expression tree for 240 − 6 × (18 + 7) and an estimate of 90](/static/course/mathematics/map-14-order-estimation-en.svg)

## Begin with a familiar image

A recipe changes if its steps are carried out in the wrong order. Parentheses mark the part of a mathematical instruction that must be completed first.

## Precise meaning

Do parentheses first, then powers, multiplication and division, and finally addition and subtraction. Operations at the same level proceed from left to right.

## The lesson's support signal

`meaning → model → calculation → check → explain in your own words`

read structure → estimate → calculate in order → compare with estimate

## Worked example

For `240 − 6 × (18 + 7)`, the parentheses give `25`, the product is `150`, and `240 − 150 = 90`.

## Example with a fading prompt

Change the numbers in the example and repeat the solution without copying its steps. Estimate first, calculate exactly, and check against the original condition.

## Recall without a prompt

1. Define the main idea in one sentence.
2. Rebuild the support signal from memory.
3. Solve the example with different numbers and explain why every step is valid.

## Find and correct the mistake

Turning `240 − 6 × 25` into `234 × 25` performs subtraction too early. The expression tree shows that the `6 × 25` branch must be completed first.

## Transfer to a new setting

Apply the same relationship to something you can measure yourself. Name the quantities, units, and the boundary within which the model is valid. Check the answer by a second representation or an inverse operation.

## Exercise

**Required.** Estimate and then calculate `360 ÷ (8 + 4) + 7 × 5`; compare the exact result with your estimate.

**With your own data.** Create one everyday example with the same structure and show it in words, a formula, and a check.

**Optional.** Seven days later, change the numbers and repeat without the support map.

## Open perspective

Negative numbers extend the number line to the left of zero and reveal another meaning of subtraction.
