# Lesson 3. Where does a language put grammatical meaning?

## Why this matters

“To our houses” combines a house, plurality, possession, and direction. **Grammar** is the set of rules connecting such meanings. **Morphology** is the part of grammar concerned with word structure. A morphological type names a common pattern, not a rigid label for every word.

## See the whole idea

**Analytic pattern:** in English `to our houses`, direction and possession appear in separate words. Picture cards lying next to one another. This pattern is prominent in English, Mandarin Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai, and Khmer. The `-s` in `houses` reminds us that a language can use several patterns.

**Agglutinative pattern:** Kazakh `үйлерімізге` has a sequence of meaningful parts. We insert hyphens *only to show boundaries in an analysis*: `үй-лер-іміз-ге` — house, plural, ours, toward. Ordinary spelling has no hyphens. Picture pieces of meat placed in order on a skewer. This pattern is common in Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Uzbek, Turkish, and Mongolian.

**Fusional pattern:** in Russian `столом` (“with a table”), `-ом` helps express both singular number and instrumental case. A **case** marks a word's grammatical role; here the question is “with what?”. Picture a single card bearing two instructions. Fusional traits are prominent in Russian, Polish, German, Spanish, and Lithuanian.

The skewer does not mean every root letter is fixed: Kazakh `кітап` (“book”) becomes `кітабым` (“my book”), with `п` changing to `б`. A rule can describe that change too. Programs can analyze the other patterns; the visible Kazakh sequence makes a good first teaching example.

## Recall map

Separate words → analytic. Ordered distinct word parts → agglutinative. Several meanings in one part → fusional.

## Task and check

Without looking at the map, classify `to our houses`, `үй-лер-іміз-ге`, and `столом`. Explain each decision. Name one additional language for each type.

**Check:** separate `to` and `our` → analytic; ordered `-лер`, `-іміз`, `-ге` → agglutinative; `-ом` combines meanings → fusional. Possible additional languages: Vietnamese, Kyrgyz, Polish.
