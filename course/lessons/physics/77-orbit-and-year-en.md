# Why is a calendar year longer than 365 days?

_Лид (summary):_ **Distinguish Earth's daily spin from one trip around the Sun.**

## Question and prediction

New Year returns after many sunrises, but two motions are involved. A runner can turn on the spot while moving round a track. Which motion corresponds to a day and which to a year? Give a prediction before reading the calculation.

## New words without gaps

An orbit is a body's path around another body under gravity. An orbital period is the time for one complete circuit. Earth travels around the Sun; the year tied to the recurrence of seasons is about 365.24 solar days. This is not exactly the same as Earth's orbital period measured against distant stars. An ellipse is an elongated circle-like shape; Earth's orbit is close to circular in our teaching drawing.

## Work through supplied data

With rounded numbers, four years accumulate about 4 × 0.24 = 0.96 day. That motivates calendar corrections, though the full leap-year rule is more precise than 'every fourth year'. The diagram gives one circuit and an approximate day count, not Earth's speed everywhere on the path. Orbital speed is not constant.

## Test safely

Draw the Sun and four Earth positions on a nearly circular orbit. On a separate small globe draw one daily spin. Label the two clocks 'rotation' and 'revolution'. If available, compare the dates of the same season in two neighbouring years rather than claiming the drawing measured them.

## A common mistake

Leap years do not occur because Earth stops or makes an extra orbit. They correct the fractional number of days in a year. New case: by the rough 0.24 day-per-year estimate, how many days accumulate over ten years?
