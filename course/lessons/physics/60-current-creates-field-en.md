# Why does a compass turn beside a wire?

_Лид (summary):_ **Discover a link between electricity and magnetism without shorting a battery.**

## Situation and prediction

In the sketch a compass sits beside a straight wire. With current flowing the needle turns; after switching off it returns. You already know current as charge moving per unit time. What explains an effect on the magnet with no mechanical contact? Predict what reversing the current might do.

## New words, step by step

A **conductor** carrying **electric current** produces a magnetic field around it. A conductor allows charge to move; here it is a circuit wire. **Conventional current direction** is from plus to minus in the external circuit, while electrons in metal move the other way. Around a straight wire the field has a circular direction. The drawn circles are a model, not visible air vortices.

## Working through supplied data

Supplied table: circuit off — no extra deflection; on — needle turns; polarity reversed — the turn reverses. This is qualitative, not an angle measurement. It supports a link between current and field but does not let us calculate field strength without current and distance.

## A safe check

Use only a ready-made low-voltage classroom kit with current limiting and adult supervision. A bare wire across a battery is a short circuit and can heat quickly. Without a kit, fill in predictions and check that a circuit drawing has a safe resistor or purpose-built module.

## Where the reasoning breaks

Do not say that the compass responds to the battery's charge in the same way while the circuit is open. In this experiment the current adds the noticeable field. Earth's field remains present; the needle responds to the combined influence.
