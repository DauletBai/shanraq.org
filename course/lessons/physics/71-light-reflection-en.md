# Why does a mirror show an object to one side? Draw the law of reflection

_Лид (summary):_ **Trace light rays, distinguish the normal from the surface and check angles on a square grid.**

## Question and prediction

Look into a small mirror. You see light from an object that the mirror directs into your eye; the mirror is not the object's light source. Turn the mirror and the apparent position changes. Before drawing, point out the path: object → mirror → eye. Never use sunlight or a laser for this activity.

## New words without gaps

A **light ray** is a line showing light's direction in a geometric model, not a visible thread. An **incident ray** goes towards a boundary and a **reflected ray** leaves it. The **normal** is an imaginary line perpendicular to the surface at the meeting point. The **angle of incidence** and **angle of reflection** are measured from the normal, not the mirror surface. For specular reflection the two angles are equal.

## Work through supplied data

In the supplied drawing the mirror is horizontal and the normal vertical. On an equal-square grid the ray travels from (2,2) to (4,4), then from (4,4) to (6,2). Each ray makes 45° with the normal. Equal horizontal and vertical steps confirm it. The coordinates name grid squares, not centimetres on a real mirror.

## Test safely

Put a safe small mirror on a desk. Use a pencil diagram and ordinary room light; do not shine a torch into anyone's eyes. Draw the normal at a right angle to the surface and compare angles with a protractor. Multiple mirrors require a separate path for each reflection.

## A common mistake

A common error is measuring from the mirror without saying so. In this special picture that also gives 45°, but a ray at 30° to the normal is at 60° to the surface. The mirror redirects light from the object; it does not create it.

A new ray makes 30° with the normal. At what angle to the normal does it leave? What angle does it make with the surface? Label both.
