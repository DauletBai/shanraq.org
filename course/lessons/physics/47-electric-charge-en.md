# Why does a balloon stick to a wall?

_Лид (summary):_ **Observe static electricity, separate charge from current, and start the electrical page of your lab notebook.**

## Situation and prediction

Rub a balloon on dry cloth and bring it near tiny paper pieces. Some may rise. The balloon has neither become a magnet nor acquired a battery. First predict whether skipping the rubbing would change the result. Humidity and fabric matter, so one unsuccessful trial does not disprove the effect.

## New words, step by step

**Electric charge** is a property of particles that makes them interact electrically. We label two kinds **+** and **−**; these are names, not good and bad. Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. A neutral object has zero net charge, yet its charges can shift slightly. That is why a charged balloon can attract neutral paper. **Static** means accumulated charge is not yet flowing steadily around a circuit. Rubbing can move electrons between materials; it does not create charge from nothing.

## Working through supplied data

In the diagram, neutral paper has equal total positive and negative charge. Near a negatively charged balloon, its positive side lies closer and its negative side farther away. The nearer attraction can outweigh the farther repulsion. This model explains a direction, but cannot calculate force without distance and material properties.

## A safe check

Use only a balloon, dry cloth and paper pieces. Compare unrubbed and rubbed cases at the same distance. Keep away from flammable vapour and sensitive electronics. Without materials, draw a prediction instead of inventing an observation.

## A reasoning trap

A common mistake is to assume every attracted object carries opposite net charge. Paper may be neutral: its charges shift. The next lesson distinguishes a one-off discharge from a steady current.
