# How often a wave arrives: period, frequency and wavelength

_Лид (summary):_ **Count repeated motions without confusing the time for one cycle with the distance between crests.**

## Question and prediction

Move one end of a rope steadily. When your hand repeats more often, more crests pass each second. Yet crests per second and the distance between crests are different quantities. Before calculating, decide which you would measure with a clock and which with a ruler.

## New words without gaps

An **oscillation** is repeated motion around an equilibrium position. **Period T** is the time for one complete cycle, measured in seconds. **Frequency f** is cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz), so f = 1/T. **Wavelength λ** is the distance between neighbouring crests at the same instant, measured in metres. **Wave speed v** is the distance a front travels per second. For a steady wave, v = λf.

## Work through supplied data

Supplied example: the hand completes 8 cycles in 4 s. Then f = 8/4 = 2 Hz and T = 4/8 = 0.5 s. If adjacent crests are 1.5 m apart, the front speed is v = 1.5 × 2 = 3 m/s. The diagram marks crests at x = 0, 1.5 and 3 m at one instant. Do not call the crest spacing a period: their units differ.

## Test safely

Mark a rope or use the drawing. If you cannot run the activity, label these numbers 'supplied'. For a real measurement, time many cycles together rather than trying to catch one quick movement with a stopwatch.

## A common mistake

The equation v = λf does not mean increasing frequency always increases speed. In a given medium, speed is often set approximately by the medium; wavelength then changes. Keep rope tension fixed when comparing trials.
