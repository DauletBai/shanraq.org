# Why do two magnets sometimes stick and sometimes push apart?

_Лид (summary):_ **Tell poles, field direction and drawn field lines apart.**

## Situation and prediction

Imagine bringing two bar magnets together. One orientation attracts; after a turn they repel. The answer depends on orientation as well as distance. Before looking at the map, sketch both cases and label the facing ends.

## New words, step by step

A magnet has two **poles**, conventionally called north N and south S. Like poles repel; unlike poles attract. A **magnetic field** describes the direction an effect would have on a small test magnet. A drawn **field line** is a direction guide, not a physical string. Outside a magnet we draw it from N to S; the path closes through the magnet.

## Working through supplied data

N facing S predicts attraction; N facing N predicts repulsion. Splitting an ordinary magnet does not isolate one N and one S: each piece has both poles. The number of drawn lines is not an exact count of physical 'flows'.

## A safe check

Use only small enclosed classroom magnets. Bring them together slowly on a table so fingers cannot get trapped. Predict N–S and N–N before checking. Without magnets, trace the arrows on the diagram and explain both cases in words.

## Where the reasoning breaks

Do not assume that red paint marks north on every magnet. A maker chooses paint colours; use a label or a compass. Field lines are not separate tracks: the field exists between drawn lines too.
