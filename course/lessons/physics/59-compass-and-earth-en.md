# Why does a compass needle turn without a touch?

_Лид (summary):_ **Use Earth's magnetic field as a reference and check local interference.**

## Situation and prediction

Imagine leaving the classroom for an open place. A free compass needle settles in roughly one direction. Near a steel desk or phone it may move away. Did Earth change, or did the test location change? Write two possible explanations before reading on.

## New words, step by step

A **compass** is a small magnet free to turn. Earth's **magnetic field** gives it an orientation. **Geographic north** points toward Earth's rotational axis on a map; **magnetic north** is the direction a compass indicates locally. These need not coincide. Their angular difference is **magnetic declination**. A nearby magnet or current adds another field and can interfere.

## Working through supplied data

Supplied example: the needle settles one way on a clear table, turns near a steel stand, and returns after the stand is removed. That supports a local interference explanation. One such observation cannot calculate your city's declination; location, date and a reference map matter.

## A safe check

If you have a simple compass, place it flat and wait for it to settle. Move it away from metal and a phone, then compare before and after. Do not travel somewhere unfamiliar for the exercise or use a compass as your only navigation tool. Without one, sketch two influences: Earth's field and a nearby disturbance.

## Where the reasoning breaks

It is wrong to promise that the red end points exactly to the geographic North Pole. The needle follows the local magnetic field; declination and interference matter. The needle's north magnetic pole and geographic north are distinct ideas.
