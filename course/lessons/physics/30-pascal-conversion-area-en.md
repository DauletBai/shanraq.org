# Why can square centimetres change an answer ten thousandfold?

_Лид (summary):_ **Convert area correctly and calculate pressure with consistent units. Check the arithmetic on a small piston model.**

## Story and prediction

Last lesson, pressure depended on area. A label may give that area in square centimetres, while our formula expects square metres. Silently swapping units seems minor but changes the answer by a factor of 10,000. Imagine a clear teaching piston with a 2 cm² face. Before inserting the number 2 into the formula, sketch a 1 cm by 1 cm square inside a 1 m by 1 m square.

Before reading, put your prediction in your notebook and justify it in one sentence. After calculating, return to it: a result matching your guess does not yet prove your explanation. Check which conditions were supplied and which things you actually observed.

## Words to learn

A **square centimetre** (cm²) is a square with 1 cm sides. A metre contains 100 cm across and 100 cm vertically, so 1 m² = 10,000 cm² and 1 cm² = 0.0001 m². A **piston** is a movable part that closes a cylinder’s cross-section. A **cross-section** is an imagined flat slice, here the face through which a force acts on fluid. For now we calculate its area; pressure transmission comes later.

## Check the model with numbers

Given F = 4 N and A = 2 cm² = 0.0002 m², p = 4/0.0002 = 20,000 Pa = 20 kPa. The prefix kilo means a thousand: 1 kPa = 1000 Pa. Check backwards: 20,000 Pa × 0.0002 m² = 4 N. If you enter 2 as if it were square metres, you get 2 Pa, an entirely different situation.

## A safe investigation

Draw a 2 cm by 2 cm square and label its area 4 cm². Convert this to 0.0004 m². With the same 4 N force, calculate 10,000 Pa. Check by multiplying pressure by area. Extension: why does doubling both side lengths quadruple area instead of doubling it?

## A likely mistake

Trap: treating cm² like a length and dividing by only 100. Area has two dimensions, so the conversion uses 100 × 100. Do not confuse kPa with kN: the first measures pressure, the second force.
