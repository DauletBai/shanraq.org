# How does a resistor protect a circuit?

_Лид (summary):_ **Connect voltage, current and resistance for a suitable resistor while learning the limits of Ohm’s law.**

## Situation and prediction

Imagine a corridor: a narrower passage admits fewer people under the same conditions. This only suggests a direction of change; current comes from the electric field around the circuit, not from electrons running like people. Apply the same 3 V to two model resistors and predict which carries more current.

## New words, step by step

**Resistance** R describes how strongly a component opposes current under stated conditions; its unit is the **ohm** (Ω). For an ohmic resistor at roughly constant temperature, **Ohm’s law** is I = U/R. The letter R does not make every device's resistance constant. A lamp filament changes resistance as it heats; an LED is nonlinear. Apply this equation to the resistor, not uncritically to every electronic component.

## Working through supplied data

With an ideal 3 V source and a 300 Ω resistor, I = 3/300 = 0.01 A = 10 mA. At the same voltage a 600 Ω resistor would carry 0.005 A = 5 mA. These are supplied values, not measurements. An LED current-limiting resistor must account for the voltage across the LED; simply dividing 3 V by 300 Ω does not give its current.

## A safe check

Draw two paper circuits and indicate the change in current. For a real ready-made kit, use only cells and adequately rated resistors under adult supervision. Never directly connect cell terminals with a wire; a resistor can get warm.

## A reasoning trap

A mistake is to imagine that current disappears inside resistance. The same current enters and leaves a resistor in a single series path; electrical energy becomes mainly heat.
