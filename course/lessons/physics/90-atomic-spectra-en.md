# Why does a gas have its own set of coloured lines?

_Лид (summary):_ **Connect a spectrum to gaps between allowed atomic energies.**

## Question and prediction

A prism can make a continuous rainbow, but some thin gases give separate bright lines. Why are the patterns different? Do not rush to say the gas 'has fewer colours'. Recall that a photon carries a particular energy and an atom need not allow every possible electron energy. Predict whether the pattern can tell us something about the material.

## New words without gaps

An energy level is an allowed state of an atom with a particular energy. A transition is a change from one level to another. When an atom changes from higher to lower energy, it may emit a photon. A spectrum is light distributed by wavelength or frequency; distinct lines reflect some allowed energy differences. The colour of a visible line depends on photon frequency. Levels do not prove that an electron travels on a ball-like orbit: they describe states, not tracks.

## Work through supplied data

Invented teaching atom: levels have energies 2 and 5 arbitrary units. For a 5 → 2 transition, the difference is 3 units, so the emitted photon has 3 units of energy. The reverse transition needs absorption of 3 units under suitable conditions. These are made-up arithmetic levels, not hydrogen data. On the map, horizontal lines at heights labelled 2 and 5 are linked by exactly one vertical arrow.

## Test safely

Draw two horizontal lines on graph paper, one at height 2 squares and the other at 5. Draw a downward arrow and compute 5−2. Add a third level at height 8 and list positive differences between every pair. Keep the invented model separate from measured spectra; the NIST database provides reference data for real atoms.

## A common mistake

Mistake: treating an energy level as a literal shelf five centimetres from the nucleus. The vertical axis represents energy, not distance. Transfer: what three gaps exist among levels 2, 5 and 8? Can different pairs yield the same gap? Check by subtracting.
