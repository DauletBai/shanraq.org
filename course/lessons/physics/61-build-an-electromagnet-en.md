# Can you make a magnet that switches off?

_Лид (summary):_ **Explore a coil, its core and fair comparisons of electromagnets.**

## Situation and prediction

Picture a lifting crane that releases a metal object on command. A permanent magnet is not so easily switched off. Can electric current control magnetism instead? Predict what a compass near a coil would do when its current is turned off.

## New words, step by step

A **coil** is many turns of insulated wire. The fields from those turns combine inside it. An **electromagnet** uses controlled current for its magnetic effect; a soft iron **core** can strengthen it. A **turn** is one loop of wire. The effect depends on current, turn count, core and distance; changing them all at once cannot identify one cause.

## Working through supplied data

Supplied example: at the same safe current, a 20-turn coil lifts 2 clips and a 40-turn one lifts 4. These are invented teaching numbers showing how to record a comparison, not a general law that doubling turns doubles clips. Clips vary, a core may saturate, and replacing the coil can change current.

## A safe check

Connect a real coil only in a ready-made current-limited low-voltage kit, following instructions with an adult. Never wind wire onto household mains or leave an energised coil unattended: it can heat. On paper, compare two supplied tables and identify which one actually keeps current constant.

## Where the reasoning breaks

It is wrong to claim that adding turns always increases lifting in exact proportion. More wire can raise resistance and reduce current with the same supply. Do not promise a result before measuring current.

## Transfer to a new case

Two coils each have 30 turns; one has an iron core, the other a plastic one. What do you predict and how would you isolate the effect of the core? Name at least two conditions to keep equal.
