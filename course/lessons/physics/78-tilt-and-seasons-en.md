# Why can summer be warm when Earth is farther from the Sun?

_Лид (summary):_ **Test axis tilt and daylight length instead of a distance guess.**

## Question and prediction

June is usually warm in Kazakhstan and December cold, while Australia has opposite seasons. If Earth–Sun distance alone caused seasons, should both hemispheres of one planet not warm together? Predict which model explains the difference.

## New words without gaps

A hemisphere is one half of Earth divided at the equator. Earth's axis is tilted about 23.4° from the perpendicular to its orbital plane and keeps nearly the same direction in space during a year. When the northern hemisphere tilts toward the Sun, sunlight arrives more directly and the daylight interval is longer. On average more solar energy reaches each unit of area. The southern hemisphere has the opposite conditions.

## Work through supplied data

In a supplied model, the same beam covers 10 cm² on flat paper and 20 cm² on slanted paper. For equal energy and time, each square centimetre in the second case receives half as much. This models ray geometry only; real temperatures also depend on clouds, air, oceans and delayed warming. The diagram shows northern summer in June and southern summer in December.

## Test safely

Use a globe and a stationary lamp. Mark the north-pole direction. Move the globe to opposite sides of the lamp without changing the direction the axis points within the room. Compare beam angles and the time a marked spot stays lit. Do not use the lamp as a heating experiment or stare into a bright bulb.

## A common mistake

A common error is re-aiming the axis toward the Sun at every orbital position; that would erase the seasonal change. Distance alone also fails: Earth is closer to the Sun around January. Why is July winter in Australia?
