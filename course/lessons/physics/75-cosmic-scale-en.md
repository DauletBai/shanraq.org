# Why does the Moon almost touch Earth in many drawings?

_Лид (summary):_ **Build one honest scale model and separate object size from separation.**

## Question and prediction

A textbook often puts Earth, Moon and Sun side by side. Would they fit on the page if sizes and distances used the same scale? Before calculating, predict where the Moon would be if Earth's diameter became one centimetre.

## New words without gaps

A scale is a rule matching a model length to a real length. Diameter runs through a sphere's centre from one edge to the other. The average Earth–Moon centre distance is about 384,000 km; Earth's diameter is about 12,700 km and the Moon's about 3,500 km. Average matters because the actual separation changes. A drawing without a common scale may show order but cannot give a trustworthy comparison of gaps.

## Work through supplied data

Supplied calculation: 384,000 ÷ 12,700 ≈ 30. With a model Earth 1 cm wide, the Moon's centre is about 30 cm from Earth's centre; its diameter is about 0.27 cm. Make Earth 10 cm wide and the Moon is about 3 m away, not 30 cm. Numbers are rounded. The diagram uses the first case: Earth 1 cm, Moon 0.27 cm, centre separation 30 cm.

## Test safely

Draw a 1 cm circle. Measure 30 cm from its centre and draw a second circle roughly 3 mm wide. Mark the ruler and distinguish centre-to-centre distance from the gap between surfaces. Explain why enlarging both bodies while keeping them close destroys the common scale.

## A common mistake

Mistake: an object that looks large in the sky must be large or close. Apparent angular size depends on both physical size and distance. Transfer question: if the model Earth's diameter doubles, where must the Moon's centre move? Show one proportion.
