# How does measurement become a design decision?

_Лид (summary):_ **Set a limit, choose a candidate and plan a new test.**

## Question and prediction

Imagine a paper model of a shelf with a soft barrier after 60 cm. Which covering might stop the cart sooner? We now need more than a difference between surfaces: we have a specific requirement. Never release a real cart near a table edge. The shelf is a thought experiment; tests belong in a spacious safe area.

## New words without gaps

A criterion is a measurable condition of success: a run no longer than 60 cm. A constraint is a rule to keep: the same cart, a low ramp and a safe stop. A prototype is a trial version of a design. Iteration is the cycle “version, test, change, retest”. A margin is the distance between limit and result, but mean margin cannot replace checking individual runs.

## Work through supplied data

Supplied means are 83 cm for paper and 45 cm for fabric. Paper exceeds the limit by 83 − 60 = 23 cm. Fabric has a mean margin of 60 − 45 = 15 cm. Even its longest supplied run, 46 cm, is below 60. Fabric is a candidate, not a safety certificate: real variation has not been measured.

## Test safely

Write the criterion before selecting a material. Plan at least three fresh releases on secured fabric and record every value, even inconvenient ones. If a run exceeds 60 cm, change the design and run a new series; do not edit the old table. Without equipment, make a precise plan and say plainly that no test was done.

## A common mistake

Mistake: declaring success from a 45 cm mean while hiding a new 63 cm run. Transfer: the new goal is at least 70 cm of travel. Which covering is now promising? Why has the meaning of “better” changed?
