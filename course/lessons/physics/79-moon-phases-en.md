# Where does the Moon go between full moons?

_Лид (summary):_ **A phase is the visible share of its sunlit half, not Earth's shadow.**

## Question and prediction

The Moon sometimes looks round and sometimes like a thin crescent. Take a ball and a side lamp: half the ball stays lit, yet an observer at different angles sees different shares of that half. Predict when the full bright disc becomes visible.

## New words without gaps

A lunar phase is the portion of the Moon's sunlit half visible from Earth. At new moon the Moon lies roughly between Earth and Sun; its lit side mostly faces away from us. At full moon Earth lies roughly between Sun and Moon, so we see almost all the lit side. At first and last quarter about half the visible disc is bright. A full cycle of phases relative to the Sun takes about 29.5 days, not exactly four weeks.

## Work through supplied data

In the supplied top-view diagram sunlight travels left to right: Sun left, Earth centre. Moon to Earth's left means new moon; above Earth means a quarter; to Earth's right means full moon. These are positions viewed from above, not pictures of the Moon's appearance in the sky. The quarter observer sees roughly half the visible disc lit. Actual visibility and brightness vary with location and conditions.

## Test safely

Hold a small ball at arm's length and turn relative to a fixed lamp. Watch how much of its illuminated half you see; do not use the Sun as the lamp. Draw three positions and label light source and observer. A sketch is evidence of your model, not a measurement of a real lunar phase.

## A common mistake

Mistake: a crescent is Earth's shadow. Earth's shadow normally misses the Moon; that is a special eclipse case for the next lesson. Why does a full Moon normally appear roughly opposite the Sun in the sky?
