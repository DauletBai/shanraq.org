# How does energy from a nucleus reach an electrical outlet?

_Лид (summary):_ **Build the conversion chain and mark where our model stops.**

## Question and prediction

At a thermal power station, a turbine turns a generator. If the fuel changes, must every part of the route to an outlet change? Before reading, write familiar links: heat warms a working fluid, its flow turns a turbine, and a generator converts rotation to electrical energy. Now ask where the heat originates in this example.

## New words without gaps

Nuclear fission splits a heavy nucleus into lighter parts while releasing energy and neutrons. A controlled chain reaction is a sequence of fissions whose rate is regulated. A reactor is an installation that sustains and controls this reaction while removing heat. A turbine is a machine turned by a flow; a generator converts rotation into electrical energy. A loop is a path for a heat-carrying fluid; power plants arrange loops differently. Radiation protection and radioactive materials require specialists and strict procedures.

## Work through supplied data

Schematic chain: fission → heat → warmed coolant and steam in an appropriate design → turbine rotation → generator → electricity. Steam is not made directly inside every type of reactor and need not travel to the turbine in the same loop; designs differ. The three stages on the right of the map are not a piping diagram of a real station. Energy is not created from nothing: it changes form, and some leaves for the environment as heat.

## A common mistake

Mistake: claiming a reactor directly produces electrical current, or that all released energy becomes electricity. Checkpoint: explain the whole chain to a beginner without skipping 'coolant', 'turbine' or 'generator'.
