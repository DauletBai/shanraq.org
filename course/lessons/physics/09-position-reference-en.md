# Where is the cart? Reference point and position

_Лид (summary):_ **Locate an object without confusing its position with the distance it travelled.**

![Where is the cart? Reference point and position](/static/course/physics/map-09-position-reference-en.svg)

## A scene from everyday life

Imagine a long school corridor. “I am by the door” is little help to a friend on another floor: there are many doors. Choose one entrance as a starting point and treat the direction towards the library as positive. “I am 6 m from the entrance” can now be checked. In our lab, a straight track for a toy cart will replace the corridor.

## New words, step by step

A **reference point** is the agreed place called zero. An **axis** is a chosen straight line with direction and scale. **Position** x is a signed number with a unit that locates an object relative to zero. We might have x = +6 m to the right and x = −2 m to the left. The sign names a side, not good or bad motion. The number 6 m alone says nothing about travel time or distance travelled.

## Work through the numbers

In the diagram the cart begins at x = 2 m and is later placed at x = 6 m. The initial position is x₁ = 2 m; the final one is x₂ = 6 m. The difference x₂ − x₁ = +4 m tells us the change in position, not any hidden detour. If we moved the cart by hand, we must not describe that as a measured run.

## Your paper investigation

Put a ruler’s zero at the edge of a sheet and mark 2 and 6 cm. Where would a mark “left of zero” go if the line continued? Describe the 2 cm place in words and numerals. Move a paper token out and back: a matching final place need not mean a matching journey.

## An easy mistake to make

Do not call a coordinate a distance travelled. “The cart is 6 m from the entrance” is a present place; “it travelled 6 m” describes a journey.
