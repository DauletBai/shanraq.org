## A scene from everyday life

A road sign shows 36 km/h; a lab table shows 10 m/s. The numbers look different. Do they describe the same speed? Replace kilometres with metres and hours with seconds without changing the motion itself.

## New words, step by step

1 km = 1000 m and 1 h = 3600 s. Therefore 1 km/h = 1000/3600 m/s = 1/3.6 m/s. Divide a km/h value by 3.6 to get m/s; multiply an m/s value by 3.6 to go back. The units cancel: (km/h) × (1000 m/km) × (1 h/3600 s) = m/s. This is unit replacement, not a magic rule.

## Work through the numbers

36 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 10 m/s. Check the meaning: 36 km in an hour is 36,000 m in 3600 s, or 10 m each second if the motion is steady. Reversing the step gives 10 m/s × 3.6 = 36 km/h. This is a teaching value for transport; our toy cart moves much more slowly.

## Your paper investigation

Convert 18 km/h to m/s: 18 ÷ 3.6 = 5 m/s. Convert 2 m/s to km/h: 2 × 3.6 = 7.2 km/h. Write units at every step and check by reversing the conversion.

## An easy mistake to make

Multiplying 36 by 3.6 when going to m/s gives the wrong direction. Write a fraction with units to see what cancels.
