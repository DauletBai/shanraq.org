# Why does moving a magnet deflect a meter?

_Лид (summary):_ **Introduce electromagnetic induction through a changing field, not current appearing by magic.**

## Situation and prediction

A coil is connected to a sensitive meter. A stationary nearby magnet changes the reading little. Push the magnet in and the needle moves one way; pull it out and it moves the other way. What do both motions share that stopping lacks? Predict before using the map.

## New words, step by step

**Electromagnetic induction** is the production of voltage around a loop when magnetic flux through it changes. **Magnetic flux** roughly describes how much magnetic field passes through the turns' area; it is not a flow of substance. **Voltage** can exist across an open coil; sustained **current** needs a closed path. Moving a magnet is one way to change flux; changing the field itself or moving the coil can also work.

## Working through supplied data

Supplied sequence: magnet still — 0 arbitrary divisions; pushed in — +3; pulled out — −3. The sign marks opposite deflection, not a speed in amperes. Faster motion often produces a larger voltage, but these three rows do not yield a universal equation for every coil.

## A safe check

Use only a ready-made classroom coil and small magnet, never a mains connection. Move slowly so the meter is not struck. Without equipment, use cards for three states: still, entering, leaving; label when flux changes. Do not call the cards your measurement.

## Where the reasoning breaks

It is wrong to think proximity alone produces a steady current. Induction requires changing flux. Do not confuse 'voltage exists' with 'current flows' when the path is open. The energy source here is work done moving the magnet.
