# Why can bright red light fail where faint blue light works?

_Лид (summary):_ **Learn photons and the photoelectric threshold without hazardous ultraviolet light.**

## Question and prediction

You already know frequency: how many wave cycles occur each second. Imagine light must free an electron from a surface. Will making the same colour brighter always help? First separate the number of arriving portions of light from the energy of each portion. These are different ways to change illumination.

## New words without gaps

A photon is one packet of electromagnetic energy. Higher frequency means more energy in each photon: E = h·f, where E is one photon's energy, f is frequency and h is Planck's constant. The photoelectric effect is electrons leaving a suitable material after it absorbs light. Threshold frequency is the lowest frequency at which one photon can provide enough energy to free an electron from that material. Brightness often tracks photon number, but at fixed frequency it does not increase each photon's energy.

## Work through supplied data

Invented teaching case, not a named metal: a surface has a threshold of 5 arbitrary frequency units. Frequency 4 cannot release an electron by this single-photon process even with many photons; frequency 6 may release one if absorbed. The units and values are for comparison, not measured data. The vertical line on the map is the threshold; photon count is a separate dimension.

## Test safely

Make ten cards labelled 4 and two labelled 6. Suppose one attempt at release needs one card worth at least 5. Count the cards that pass; do not merge ten fours into a single photon. This is a simple threshold model, not an actual experiment. Do not use UV lamps, lasers or exposed electrical circuits.

## A common mistake

Mistake: substituting total brightness for one photon's energy. Real materials also reflect, absorb and lose energy; cards omit those effects. Transfer: if frequency doubles, what happens to one photon's energy under E = h·f? What if only the number of photons at the original frequency doubles?
