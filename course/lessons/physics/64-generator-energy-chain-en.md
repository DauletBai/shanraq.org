# Does a generator make electricity from nothing?

_Лид (summary):_ **Trace mechanical work, changing flux and electrical energy without breaking energy conservation.**

## Situation and prediction

A bicycle dynamo lamp shines while the wheel turns. Pedal harder and both turning and light may change. What happens to the lamp when the wheel stops? Predict and name the energy source before using the word 'generator'.

## New words, step by step

A **generator** converts mechanical work into electrical energy using induction. Turning a coil or magnet changes **magnetic flux**. **Alternating voltage** changes magnitude or direction with time; a **rectifier** can convert it when a circuit needs one current direction. **Power** is energy per second, not energy stored inside a coil.

## Working through supplied data

Supplied budget over an arbitrary interval: pedalling transfers 100 J of mechanical energy; the lamp receives 60 J; 40 J becomes heating and other losses. 60/100 = 60% for this imaginary system. These are not bicycle specifications. The generator did not make 100 J from nowhere: a person supplied them through the pedals.

## A safe check

The safe option is a ready-made hand-cranked teaching generator used as instructed, or a paper energy chain. Do not connect a homemade generator to household wiring or touch rotating parts. In the notebook, distinguish supplied figures from anything you actually observed.

## Where the reasoning breaks

Calling the generator a 'creator' of energy is wrong: it is a converter. It is also wrong to assume that every rotating generator always outputs steady voltage of one polarity; the output depends on design and electronics.
