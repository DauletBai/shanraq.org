# Why does a pencil look broken in water? Light at a boundary

_Лид (summary):_ **Separate changes of direction and speed from the frequency that stays fixed at a boundary.**

## Question and prediction

A pencil dipped into a clear glass of water looks displaced at the surface. The pencil is whole: the light path from its submerged part to your eye changes. Describe what you see before explaining it. The glass has not changed the object's actual shape.

## New words without gaps

**Refraction** is a change in the direction of light when it passes between media and its speed changes. The **boundary** here is the water surface. The normal is again perpendicular to it. From air into water an angled ray usually bends towards the normal; from water into air it bends away. Frequency does not change at the boundary, while speed and wavelength do. A ray along the normal keeps its direction even though its speed changes.

## Work through supplied data

The supplied diagram has an incident angle of 45° to the normal and a transmitted angle of about 32° in water. These are teaching values, not a student's glass measurement. Compare each line to the vertical normal: the water angle is smaller. Do not calculate a refractive index from a sketch before learning Snell's law and the required conditions.

## Test safely

Use a pencil and an ordinary smooth-edged glass. Look from the side and above while keeping the pencil still. Draw rays on paper; sunlight, lasers and electrical gear near water are unnecessary. Record that the apparent displacement is the seen position, not a break in the object.

## A common mistake

It is wrong to say the surface pushes the pencil or that the light's frequency changes on entering water. It is also wrong to expect a change of direction for a ray arriving exactly along the normal. Its speed changes, but its symmetric path remains straight.
