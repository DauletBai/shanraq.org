# How does the Sun warm us across space?

_Лид (summary):_ **The third energy-transfer process is radiation. Explore it without dangerous lamps or heaters.**

## A real-life question and prediction

On a clear day, one side of a bench is sunlit and the other shaded. Air near both may be similar in temperature, yet the surfaces differ. Where does the extra energy on the lit side come from? Space between Sun and Earth is nearly empty, so air circulation alone cannot explain it. Predict what happens if an opaque screen shades a surface.

## Words to understand

**Thermal radiation** transfers energy by electromagnetic waves; no material is needed along a path through empty space. **Absorption** means a surface receives energy; **reflection** sends some incoming radiation away. The Sun emits different kinds of radiation, and Earth’s surface also emits radiation. Opaque shade reduces direct solar input but need not stop all energy received from air and surrounding objects.

## A worked example

The teaching map has the Sun as source, even vacuum along the route, radiation as the transfer, and shade reducing direct flow. No exact temperature follows from those four labels: we also need illumination, surface properties, time and energy losses. Therefore “black cloth is always hotter than white cloth” without equal test conditions is too strong a claim.

## Test it safely

Place two identical sheets of paper side by side, one in sunlight and one in shade, and describe the conditions. Do not touch very hot surfaces, stare at the Sun or use a powerful lamp. Compare measured surface temperatures only if a thermometer is suited to the surface and the wait time is identical. Otherwise record a qualitative observation.

## An easy mistake to make

Mistake: treating radiation as warm air that travelled from the Sun. There is no such air bridge through nearly empty space. Shade does not automatically make an object colder than surrounding air, either: other exchange processes remain.
