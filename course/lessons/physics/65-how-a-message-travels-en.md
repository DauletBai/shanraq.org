# How can a message arrive without moving paper?

_Лид (summary):_ **Build the signal route from information and code to a physical change and a receiver's reconstruction.**

## Situation and prediction

A friend wants to send 'YES' through a wall. Agree that one short flashlight blink means Y and two mean E; a different pattern can stand for S. Transmission works only if both people know the rule. Phones replace the light with electrical and electromagnetic signals. First invent a code and see whether a partner can read it.

## New words, step by step

**Information** is distinguishable message content. A **code** maps content to a sequence of symbols. A **signal** is a physical change carrying those symbols, such as changing wire voltage or an electromagnetic field. A **transmitter** creates a signal; a **receiver** detects and decodes it. Radio uses changing fields; we study the nature of waves in the next block.

## Working through supplied data

Teaching code: Y = 10, E = 01. 'YE' becomes 1001. Four code symbols do not mean four English letters or four electrons. Losing one symbol could produce a different message. Real systems add timing and error-detection rules, whose purpose we can name without building them yet.

## A safe check

Use cards or paper 'pulses': one learner encodes, the other cannot see the original word and decodes using a pre-agreed table. No radio equipment or phone data is needed. If messages differ, record where the mismatch arose and change one rule at a time.

## Where the reasoning breaks

A magnet or electron does not contain a word. Meaning lies in the agreed code and sequence of changes. Do not confuse how fast signal energy travels with how fast individual electrons drift in a wire.
