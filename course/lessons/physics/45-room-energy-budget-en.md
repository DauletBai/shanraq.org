# Why can a room cool with the window shut?

_Лид (summary):_ **Build a simple energy budget for a room, then ask what it cannot tell us about temperature.**

## A real-life question and prediction

The window is shut and heating turned off, yet the room becomes cooler. Closed does not mean perfectly insulated: energy can pass through glass, walls and door gaps. Draw a boundary around the room and choose a time interval. What enters and what leaves? Do not confuse that with guessing an air temperature without knowing the air mass and heat capacity.

Pause before the explanation: what exactly do you expect to see, and why? Write a prediction before calculating. After the lesson, separate what you observed, what you calculated from supplied numbers, and what you only inferred about invisible particles. That record lets you correct a model without replacing evidence with a neat answer.

## Words to understand

Our **system** is the selected room, including air and objects; a **boundary** separates it from outside and neighbouring rooms. An **energy budget** compares all inputs and outputs over the same interval. **Insulation** slows energy transfer; it does not create energy or necessarily remove all losses. Conduction and radiation can cross a window, and moving air can pass through gaps. Label each path honestly.

## A worked example

Supplied teaching data for one interval: heating adds 600 kJ, 200 kJ leave through the window and 100 kJ through the door. Thus ΔU = 600 − 200 − 100 = +300 kJ, assuming other flows are negligible. This is internal-energy change of the chosen system, not a temperature rise of 300 degrees. Nor does it prove that only air gained energy: furniture and walls are included.

## Test it safely

Audit the room on paper. Outline it, label the input and two outputs, then add a possible third route through the ceiling. Recalculate if another 50 kJ leaves by that route. No experiments with wiring, outlets or heating appliances are needed. If observing a real room, record only safe room-temperature readings and the time.

## An easy mistake to make

Mistake: treating a closed window as perfect insulation or calling the calculated 300 kJ a measurement. Keep joules and kilojoules apart: 600 kJ is 600,000 J. The balance applies only to the chosen boundary and interval.
