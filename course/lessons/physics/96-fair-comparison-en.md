# Did the surface alone cause the difference?

_Лид (summary):_ **Compare both series and look for rival causes.**

## Question and prediction

Mean distance is 83 cm on paper and 45 cm on fabric. The difference is large. But imagine lowering the ramp or letting a wheel rub the axle before the second series. Distance could change without any fabric effect. A good investigation seeks evidence and rival explanations.

## New words without gaps

A fair comparison changes one chosen condition while keeping other important conditions alike. A rival explanation is another factor that could cause the outcome. Control does not mean making everything in the world perfectly equal: name likely influences and check them. Wheel friction, air resistance and a crooked axle may all act; one distance does not measure their separate effects.

## Work through supplied data

In the supplied data, 83 − 45 = 38 cm. Individual runs are also separated: paper 82–84 cm, fabric 44–46 cm. Under the stated conditions this agrees with a shorter run on fabric. It does not yield a friction coefficient or promise exactly 38 cm with another wheel or covering.

## Test safely

Make a control table: cart, release height, ramp angle, zero mark, wheel condition and covering width. Write how each was held alike. If you use only supplied data, clearly say that these controls were planned, not actually carried out by you.

## A common mistake

Mistake: saying “fabric has 38 cm more friction”: centimetres measure distance, not force. Transfer: fabric snags an axle differently on each run. How would you secure it, and how could you detect the defect?
