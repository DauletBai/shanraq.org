## A question from daily life

From a window you see two playground swings moving. Why does one seem faster? Before answering, decide what 'faster' means and how you could compare them.

## Words and meanings

**Physics** studies natural phenomena through observation, measurement, and explanations that can be checked. A **phenomenon** is a noticeable change, such as a moving swing. A **testable question** states what you will change or compare and what you will observe. 'Why is the world like this?' is too broad; 'does a swing with a longer rope take more time for one complete trip?' can be checked. A **model** is a simplified description useful for one question, not a complete copy of the world.

## Predict before checking

Predict what could be measured with two rope lengths: the number of swings in the same time, or the time for the same number of swings? Either works if you name the result precisely.

## Do it and record it

Draw a swing with a short rope and one with a long rope. In your notebook write the question, your expected result, and a way to check it. Do not hang anything yet: first learn to ask a clear question.

## A worked example

Vague: 'Which swing is good?' Testable: 'With the same small starting tilt, which swing takes longer for ten complete swings?' The comparison, count, and observable result are now clear. The answer is still unknown.

## A common mistake

'The longer swing is slower because physics says so' treats an expectation as evidence. No experiment has happened yet. Keep 'I predict' separate from 'I observed'.

## A new situation

A wide glass and a narrow glass of water stand in a warm room. Write a testable question about cooling. What would you measure if an adult later approved the experiment? Do not handle hot water for this exercise.

## Notebook entry and self-check

Write three lines: question, what could change, what could be measured. Could another person identify the two cases you intend to compare? If not, make the question more precise.
