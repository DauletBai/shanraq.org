# Prediction and experiment: change one condition

_Лид (summary):_ **Compare two cases so a result can be linked to the condition you changed.**

## A question from daily life

Two people time swings. The first has a short rope and a strong push; the second has a long rope and a weak push. If the times differ, which difference mattered? We cannot tell from this comparison.

## Words and meanings

An **experimental condition** is something we set, such as rope length. The **changed condition** is the one difference we want to investigate. **Held conditions** are things we try to keep the same. A **prediction** is an expected result written before observation. A **hypothesis** is a testable proposed explanation; it may be wrong. Agreement with a result does not prove it forever.

## Predict before checking

Which takes longer for ten complete swings: a 30 cm or a 60 cm pendulum? Record your answer before seeing any times. It is only a prediction so far.

## Do it and record it

Plan on paper: use the same lightweight weight, count ten swings each time, start from a small tilt without pushing, and start the timer in the same way. Change only the distance from the hanging point to the weight's centre: 30 cm and 60 cm. Actual measurement comes later.

## A worked example

Unfair comparison: count 10 swings at 30 cm but 20 at 60 cm. Fair comparison: count 10 at both lengths. Worked values of 11.0 and 15.6 seconds do not establish that their ratio applies under every condition.

## A common mistake

'I changed one variable, so everything else was identical' is too strong. A person's reaction when starting the timer and moving air can still vary. That is why we repeat measurements.

## A new situation

Two equal damp towels dry, one in shade and one near a window. Which conditions are hard to hold fixed? Do not hang anything on a heater for this exercise.

## Notebook entry and self-check

Record your prediction, the one changed condition, three held conditions, and one possible unwanted difference. Was the prediction written before the result?
