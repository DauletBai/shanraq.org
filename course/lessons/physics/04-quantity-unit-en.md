## A question from daily life

A friend asks you to cut a string '30' long. Thirty what: millimetres, centimetres, or metres? A bare number does not specify a physical length.

## Words and meanings

A **physical quantity** is a property that can be compared and measured by an agreed method, such as length or time. A **unit** is the agreed size for comparison. Length may be measured in centimetres (cm) or metres (m), time in seconds (s). A **quantity value** combines a number and a unit: 30 cm. Since 1 m = 100 cm, 30 cm = 0.30 m. The numbers differ, but the two expressions name the same length.

## Predict before checking

Which is longer, 30 cm or 0.5 m? Before calculating, put both lengths into one unit and write your prediction.

## Do it and record it

Find a ruler. Mark 0 cm, 10 cm, and 30 cm on paper; if the ruler is short, move it end to end with no gap. Label every number with a unit. A stopwatch will be useful later.

## A worked example

0.5 m = 50 cm, so 50 cm exceeds 30 cm by 20 cm. Check the meaning: half a metre is half of 100 centimetres. '30 cm = 0.30 m' writes one length in two ways.

## A common mistake

'30 cm = 30 m' is false: equal numbers with different units need not be equal quantities. '10 swings = 10 seconds' is also false: swings are counted, seconds measure time.

## A new situation

One note says 'time for ten swings: 11.0'; another says '11.0 s'. Which can enter a table without guessing? Repair the first note.

## Notebook entry and self-check

Make a tiny notebook dictionary: length → cm or m; time → s. Convert 30 cm to metres and 0.6 m to centimetres. Answers: 0.30 m and 60 cm.
