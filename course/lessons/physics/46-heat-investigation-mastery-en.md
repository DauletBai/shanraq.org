# Why does a balloon stick to a wall?

_Лид (summary):_ **Use the particle model, three transfer processes and an energy budget in one safe design project.**

## A real-life question and prediction

Imagine a teaching room in winter. Its owner wants to reduce energy loss without changing wiring or buying an expensive device. Propose one safe change: sealing a gap, using a thick curtain, or something similar. First phrase a testable question: “Will this reduce window losses over the same hour?” Predict the direction of change and state what would have to be measured to verify it.

Pause before the explanation: what exactly do you expect to see, and why? Write a prediction before calculating. After the lesson, separate what you observed, what you calculated from supplied numbers, and what you only inferred about invisible particles. That record lets you correct a model without replacing evidence with a neat answer.

## Words to understand

A **success criterion** is fixed before testing, such as smaller calculated loss under the same external conditions. A **constraint** is a rule that must hold: safety, budget, free exit from the room. **Iteration** means revising a design after checking it. Keep three levels of evidence apart: a numerical example, a temperature observation and real energy savings. Use the same system boundary and time interval for comparisons.

## A worked example

Baseline teaching figures for one interval: 600 kJ enter, 200 kJ leave through the window and 100 kJ through the door; remainder +300 kJ. In a hypothetical redesign, the window loss becomes 150 kJ while everything else is equal. The new remainder is 600 − 150 − 100 = +350 kJ, or 50 kJ more left in the system. This is a model comparison. Without actual energy readings, neither money saved nor degrees gained can be claimed.

## Test it safely

Build a notebook page with room diagram, prediction, two cases in identical units, calculation, constraint and conclusion. A safe observation is to compare room temperature at the same time on several days, noting weather and heating state. Do not block ventilation or exits or touch wiring. If a fair comparison is impossible, present the work as a design using supplied numbers.

## An easy mistake to make

Mistake: attributing the modelled 50 kJ to a real window without measurements, or calling +350 kJ a temperature. “Improving” insulation by blocking ventilation is unsafe and changes the task. A good conclusion includes the conditions behind its number.
