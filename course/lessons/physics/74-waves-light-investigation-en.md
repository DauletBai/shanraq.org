# Checkpoint: design an optical signal for a door

_Лид (summary):_ **Bring together a light path, reflection and an honest test of a safe model device.**

## Question and prediction

Lesson 66 let you encode 'door open' as an electrical signal. Now design a paper model: an LED module points at a small mirror on a moving door, and a fixed screen shows a light spot. Turning the door changes the reflected direction. Predict which of two positions puts the spot inside a drawn target.

## New words without gaps

An **optical signal** carries a message by light. A **position sensor** here is only the chosen rule 'spot in target = door closed'; a drawing does not become a reliable security device. A **controlled condition** is something kept unchanged in a comparison: source, mirror, screen and distances. As in the previous block, people choose a message code; a physical quantity does not say 'closed' on its own.

## Work through supplied data

Supplied table: with the door closed the mirror normal is vertical, and a ray incident at 30° reflects at 30° to the normal, making a spot on target. When the door opens, the mirror turns 10°. If the incoming ray stays fixed, the reflected ray rotates by 20° in the ideal plane-mirror model, moving the spot outside the target. The diagram shows angles, not exact millimetres at the screen. Mirror turn and reflected-ray turn are not equal.

## Test safely

First run a paper test: draw both mirror positions, normals and rays. If desired, use a ready low-power LED module and small safe mirror with adult supervision; use no laser, sunlight or mains equipment. Repeat several closures, recording hits and false signals instead of one success.

## A common mistake

One hit does not prove reliability. Repeat trials, secure the mount and check changing ambient light. Light also does not need air like sound; it crosses empty space. Without a screen distance, the spot's shift cannot be stated in centimetres.
