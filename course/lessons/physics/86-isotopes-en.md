# How can two carbon atoms be different?

_Лид (summary):_ **Hold the proton count fixed and change only the neutrons.**

## Question and prediction

Two cards both say 'carbon', yet one model nucleus contains more pieces. Must a card be wrong? Recall yesterday's three clues: protons define the element, neutrons have no charge, and changing electrons need not change the element. Predict which change can make two atoms of the same element different.

## New words without gaps

Isotopes are atoms of one element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. Mass number counts protons plus neutrons in the nucleus; it is not a precise mass in grams. C-12 means carbon with mass number 12. The nucleus is the atom's central region containing protons and neutrons. A stable isotope does not spontaneously decay; an unstable one may change. We will define 'radioactive' in the next lesson.

## Work through supplied data

Compare supplied cards. C-12 has 6 protons and 6 neutrons, while C-14 has 6 protons and 8 neutrons. Thus 6 + 6 = 12 and 6 + 8 = 14. Both have atomic number 6 because atomic number means proton count. As neutral atoms both have 6 electrons. The drawing does not claim a true scale for the nuclei; its symbols are for counting only.

## Test safely

Make two paper nucleus cards and six separate electron cards for each. In a table list protons, neutrons, mass number, electrons and net charge. Compare proton counts first, then neutron counts. Do not attempt to make or extract isotopes from materials at home: counting paper cards is all this task requires.

## A common mistake

Mistake: thinking C-14 has fourteen protons. Fourteen counts both kinds of nuclear particle; the proton count stays six. Transfer: how would you label an atom with 6 protons and 7 neutrons? How many electrons make it neutral? Explain each step.
