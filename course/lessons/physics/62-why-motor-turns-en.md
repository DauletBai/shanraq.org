# How can a magnet and current turn a motor?

_Лид (summary):_ **Connect force on a current-carrying wire to rotation and trace where the motion's energy comes from.**

## Situation and prediction

In a toy car the battery stays still while the wheel turns. A motor sits between them. What inside converts electrical energy into motion? Before reading, sketch 'battery → current → magnetic effect → rotation' and mark where some energy may become heat.

## New words, step by step

A wire carrying current in a magnetic field can experience a **force**. Forces on opposite sides of a rotating coil produce **torque**, an effect that turns an axle. The **rotor** turns and the **stator** stays still. In a simple brushed motor a **commutator** changes the coil connection every half-turn so turning continues in the same direction. This is a qualitative model; real motors have losses.

## Working through supplied data

Supplied energy budget: a battery transfers 10 J, 6 J becomes useful mechanical work, and 4 J becomes heating and other losses. Useful fraction 6/10 = 0.6, or 60%. This is not the rated efficiency of all motors. A stalled motor can still draw current and heat its windings.

## A safe check

Study a transparent teaching model or a diagram instead of constructing a motor. Identify the coil, magnet, axle and power source, then trace one half-turn. Use a ready-made low-voltage kit only as instructed and supervised; do not open mains appliances or stop a spinning motor with your hand.

## Where the reasoning breaks

A permanent magnet does not 'fuel' the motor. It supplies a field; the battery supplies electrical energy, some of which becomes motion. A fixed pair of forces alone does not guarantee endless rotation without commutation or another design.
