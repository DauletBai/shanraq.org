# Why does a steel ship float while a steel ball sinks?

_Лид (summary):_ **Connect average density, displaced-water volume and force balance. A ship does not break Archimedes’ principle.**

## Story and prediction

A steel ship can float while a small solid steel ball sinks. Looking only at the material misleads us: a hull encloses air, spreading the whole ship’s mass over a large outer volume. To float, it displaces enough water for buoyancy to balance its weight. If overloaded beyond the volume it can safely displace, it begins to sink.

## Words to learn

**Average density** of the whole body is total mass divided by outer volume, including hollows inside the hull. Do not confuse it with the density of steel. **Floating equilibrium** means upward buoyant force equals downward gravitational force. **Draft** is how far the hull lies below the waterline. More cargo requires more displaced water and usually a greater draft.

## Check the model with numbers

Worked model: a little boat and cargo have mass 0.3 kg. At g ≈ 10 N/kg, their weight is 3 N. Equilibrium in water requires V = 3/(1000 × 10) = 0.0003 m³ = 0.3 L of displaced water. One litre is the volume of a 10 cm by 10 cm by 10 cm cube: 1 L = 0.001 m³. If the boat’s outer volume is too small, that static floating state is impossible.

## A safe investigation

Fold a small foil boat and place it in a shallow tray of water. Add identical light coins one at a time and sketch the draft. Do not turn a visual observation into an invented force measurement. Stop before water spills. With no experiment, calculate the displaced volume for 0.5 kg: 0.5 L. Why does a wide hollow shape help keep a larger air volume?

## A likely mistake

Trap: “steel becomes lighter than water”. Steel density does not change; we consider the average density of the whole air-filled hull. An empty and an overloaded boat are different systems.
