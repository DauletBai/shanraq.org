# Why do your palms warm when you rub them?

_Лид (summary):_ **Internal energy can change by heating and by work. Learn to count energy entering and leaving a system.**

## A real-life question and prediction

Rub your palms for a few seconds. They become warm without a nearby heater or sunlight. Moving them takes effort; friction converts some motion energy into internal energy of skin and air. Predict whether the palms remain warm forever in a cool room after you stop. To explain, set a boundary: what counts as “the palms”, and what is the surrounding environment?

Pause before the explanation: what exactly do you expect to see, and why? Write a prediction before calculating. ## Words to understand

**Internal energy** is energy associated with the motion and interaction of an object’s particles. **Heat** Q means energy transferred because of a temperature difference; it is not a substance stored inside. **Work** W is energy transferred when a force acts through a displacement. The joule (J) is an energy unit. If W means work done on the object, then ΔU = Q + W; negative Q means energy leaves by heating. Explain the sign instead of guessing it.

## A worked example

Teaching data: 50 J of work is done on an object; it receives 20 J as heat and releases 30 J as heat. Hence ΔU = 50 + 20 − 30 = +40 J. This is an internal-energy change, not necessarily a thermometer reading: material state and amount also matter. If W instead meant work done by the object, its sign would reverse.

## Test it safely

Rub your palms and then let them cool. Record a sensation and the time, but assign no numerical temperature without an instrument. Draw a boundary around your palms and arrows showing work entering and energy leaving for cooler air. Do not use heaters or rub your skin until it hurts.

## An easy mistake to make

Mistake: writing “the palms contain 20 J of heat”. Heat names a transfer process, while internal energy describes a state. Always state which sign represents energy entering and leaving in a numerical balance.
