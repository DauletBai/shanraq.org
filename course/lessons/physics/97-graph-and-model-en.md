# How do we graph data without misleading people?

_Лид (summary):_ **Draw a chart with correct units and a zero-based scale.**

## Question and prediction

If bars of 83 and 45 cm begin at 40 cm, the first appears many times taller. The numbers are unchanged, but the picture misleads. A chart is for comparison, not decoration. Are paper and fabric numbers on a continuous scale or names of two groups?

## New words without gaps

A category is a group name such as “paper”. Separate bars suit categories. The measured quantity is distance to rest in centimetres; a scale shows its values. Bar lengths should be counted from zero. A model is a simplified view of one aspect of reality. This chart shows mean distance, not the cart’s speed at every instant. Joining categories with a line does not reveal measured intermediate surfaces.

## Work through supplied data

Draw an axis “distance to rest, cm” from 0 to 90. Bars: paper 83, fabric 45 cm. The 38 cm difference is a calculation, not a third trial. Thin marks may show observed ranges 82–84 and 44–46 cm; do not call these a proven confidence interval.

## Test safely

On squared paper let one large square mean 10 cm. Label the axis, unit, both coverings and title. Show the chart to someone who has not read the lesson: can they recover the values? A two-row text table is an accessible alternative that does not depend on colour.

## A common mistake

Mistake: joining the surface names with a line as though a continuous transition was measured. Transfer: a third covering is added. What changes in the chart? Why is the 83 cm bar not a speed?
