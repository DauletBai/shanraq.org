# Why is a room heater placed near the floor?

_Лид (summary):_ **Moving air carries energy along with matter. Distinguish convection from conduction.**

## A real-life question and prediction

In a heated room, warm air near the heater may rise while cooler air sinks towards it. This forms a circulation, not a rule that “heat always goes up”. Draw a room section with a window and heater. Where would you expect cooler air? Refine the question: what changes when the window is open and wind sets the air motion?

## Words to understand

**Convection** is energy transfer by a moving gas or liquid. A **flow** is directed motion of matter; this separates convection from conduction. Air generally expands on warming and is less dense under comparable conditions. Gravity and pressure then contribute to motion, while room shape, ventilation and wind can change the pattern. A drawing cannot replace measuring a real room.

## A worked example

In the teaching picture, air near the heater is 25 °C and distant air 18 °C. The warm region may rise and carry energy upward; cooler air returns below. The 7 °C difference does not determine flow speed or joules: room dimensions, time and amount of air are missing. When the heater is switched off, this direction can eventually disappear.

## Test it safely

Hang two light paper strips safely at different room locations, away from hot surfaces and fans. Record whether they move alike. A strong draught prevents a clean test of natural convection. Without an experiment, trace the loop on your room drawing and identify where the material itself moves.

## An easy mistake to make

Mistake: insisting warm air “wants to rise” under every condition. Wind or a fan can drive it elsewhere. Another mistake is drawing an energy arrow but omitting the motion arrow for the air itself.
