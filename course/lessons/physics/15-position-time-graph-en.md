## A scene from everyday life

We observe a cart every two seconds. Instead of a long account—“first here, then there”—we write pairs of numbers. A graph shows the order: each point answers “where was it at this time?” It does not draw the real shape of the track.

## New words, step by step

A **position-time graph** x(t) plots position x against time t. The horizontal axis is time in seconds; the vertical one is position in metres. A **data pair** (t, x) belongs to one observation. A line between measured points may be a model, but without extra observations it does not prove that motion followed that line.

## Work through the numbers

Worked data: (0 s, 0 m), (2 s, 4 m), (4 s, 8 m). The points lie on one straight line. If we assume steady motion between them, position increases by 4 m every 2 s. Yet three snapshots cannot rule out a brief stop between snapshots.

## Your paper investigation

Draw the axes and points. Add a fourth worked point (6 s, 8 m): position did not change from 4 to 6 s, so the cart may have stood still. Label the horizontal stretch a model consistent with data, not a continuous video record.

## An easy mistake to make

Do not read a sloping line as a sloping track. The vertical axis represents position along the chosen line, not the height above the floor.
