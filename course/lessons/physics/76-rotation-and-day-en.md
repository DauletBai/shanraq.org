# Does the Sun cross the sky, or do we turn with Earth?

_Лид (summary):_ **Separate apparent daily motion from Earth's rotation.**

## Question and prediction

The Sun appears in the eastern sky in the morning and leaves in the west. On a merry-go-round, still surroundings seem to move. Could an observer's rotation explain this familiar sky pattern? Predict before turning the model.

## New words without gaps

The rotation axis is an imagined line through Earth's poles. Rotation is turning about an axis; one full turn is 360°. Earth rotates from west to east. The Sun and stars therefore appear to move in the opposite direction. A solar day is about 24 hours. The exact apparent motion of the Sun also depends on Earth's yearly orbit.

## Work through supplied data

In a simple model, 360° ÷ 24 h = 15° per hour. This is an average turning angle per solar day, not a promise that each object moves along the horizon by exactly 15° each hour. In the diagram Earth turns east and the apparent sky motion points west. Only the hemisphere facing the lamp is lit.

## Test safely

Mark your location on a globe or ball. Put a stationary lamp to one side and turn the globe from west to east. Record when the mark enters and leaves the lit half. Never shine a bright lamp into eyes. Write what the model explains and what it does not yet explain, such as seasonal changes in day length.

## A common mistake

A common mistake is attributing day and night to Earth's yearly trip around the Sun. That motion is much slower than the daily turn. New case: if a model globe makes a full turn in 12 hours, how long is a quarter turn?
