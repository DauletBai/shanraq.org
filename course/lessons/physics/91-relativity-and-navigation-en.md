# Why can a satellite clock not simply copy time on Earth?

_Лид (summary):_ **Add two measured GPS corrections and learn the limits of the classroom model.**

## Question and prediction

A navigation receiver finds position from travel times of radio signals sent by satellites. Even a tiny clock error changes the inferred distance. Recall the earlier blocks: speed is distance per time, a radio wave carries a signal, and a satellite follows an orbit. Predict whether a moving clock high above Earth will keep exactly the same time as a clock on the ground.

## New words without gaps

Relativity is a well-tested physical theory of space, time and motion. Two effects matter when comparing satellite and ground clocks. Orbital motion makes a satellite clock run slightly slower; the different gravitational field at altitude makes it run faster. A microsecond, µs, is one millionth of a second. 'Faster' and 'slower' compare clock readings after accounting for motion and location; they do not mean a clock is broken.

## Work through supplied data

NASA gives approximate GPS values: motion contributes −7 µs per day, while the gravitational difference contributes +45 µs per day. Their sum is −7 + 45 = +38 µs per day relative to ground clocks. The plus sign means the satellite clock would get ahead without the needed correction. This is not a number for every satellite; different heights and speeds change the effects. The diagram puts both contributions and the net change on one number line. Earth size and orbital altitude are not to scale.

## Test safely

On graph paper, move seven squares left from zero and forty-five squares right. The result is thirty-eight squares to the right. Explain why 'it moves, so its clock must run slower' is incomplete: the second effect is larger. Only paper and arithmetic are needed, not a receiver or precision clock.

## A common mistake

Mistake: adding 7 and 45 without signs, or assigning +38 µs/day to every spacecraft. Transfer: in an invented case motion contributes −12 and altitude +20 units. What is the net change? Why is that no substitute for a real GPS calculation? Separate invented figures from NASA's approximate GPS values.
