## A scene from everyday life

Two people use the same path. One covers 12 m in 4 s and the other in 6 s. Who moves faster? You do not need a wearable instrument: find how many metres correspond to one second. That gives a simple measure of how fast motion is.

## New words, step by step

**Speed** here is distance travelled per unit of time; for steady motion v = s / Δt. **Steady motion** covers equal distances in equal time intervals over the part being studied. The letter v means speed, s distance, and Δt duration. The unit m/s is read “metres per second.” If the motion is not steady, the quotient gives only average speed over the whole interval.

## Work through the numbers

A worked cart covers s = 12 m in Δt = 4 s. v = 12 m / 4 s = 3 m/s. For steady motion it covers 3 m during each second. Check by reversing the calculation: 3 m/s × 4 s = 12 m. A toy cart may be too slow for 3 m/s; these are teaching numbers, not measurements from our setup.

## Your paper investigation

On paper make a table for steady motion at 3 m/s: at 0, 1, 2, 3, and 4 s, distance is 0, 3, 6, 9, and 12 m. Explain why this table would not fit a cart that stopped halfway.

## An easy mistake to make

Do not write “3 m” for “3 m/s.” The first is a length; the second is a distance-to-time ratio. One overall result does not reveal speed at every instant.
