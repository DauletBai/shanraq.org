# The sky as a lab: explain a day, a season and a Moon phase

_Лид (summary):_ **Build a testable investigation instead of a memorised list.**

## Question and prediction

A friend says: 'Summer is warmer because Earth is closer to the Sun; a crescent is Earth's shadow.' Do not appeal to authority. Build three small models, each testing a different part of the claim: rotation, axial tilt and the Moon's illuminated half.

## New words without gaps

A claim is a statement testable with an observation or calculation. A model is a simplified representation preserving the relevant relationship. A limit is what the model does not show. A source identifies where a number or observation came from. Honest conclusions separate your own observation from a supplied calculation and a prediction. Agreement with one example does not prove that a model explains the whole sky.

## Work through supplied data

Supplied reference values: Earth turns about once per day; its axis tilts about 23.4°; lunar phases repeat after about 29.5 days; sunlight arrives after about eight minutes. These numbers explain different things and do not belong in one formula. Four cards in the diagram follow the reasoning order: question → model → observable consequence → limit. Each row must address one phenomenon, not claim an answer to everything.

## Test safely

Make a lab notebook table: phenomenon, prediction, light-direction drawing, observable check, source and limit. For a day, mark a spot on a rotating globe. For seasons, compare hemispheres while keeping the axis direction fixed. For a phase, show the bright half of a ball. All work can be done on paper; do not invent an observation you did not make.

## A common mistake

Review another learner's explanation. If a phase is called Earth's shadow, ask why eclipses do not happen monthly. If a season is blamed on distance, ask them to compare north and south in July. Keep two questions that these models leave open for the next block.

## Check your understanding

Close the text and explain the left-to-right drawing to a new learner. Name an observation, a teaching assumption and something the evidence does not prove. Then answer the transfer question without a hint and check units or direction against the diagram. If you disagree, return to the first unfamiliar word rather than memorising a sentence. A spoken explanation, a drawing and a measurement are different kinds of evidence: label each honestly.

## Lab notebook page

Add a line to My World Lab Notebook: question → prediction → controlled conditions → changed condition → observation or calculation → conclusion → limit. Put a scale on a drawing or mark it 'schematic'. If you ran no experiment, write 'supplied calculation', not 'we measured'. Revisit the entry after the next lesson and note what became clearer.
