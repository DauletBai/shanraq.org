# Why will a lamp not shine with one wire?

_Лид (summary):_ **Build a first closed low-voltage circuit and read its diagram without touching a mains outlet.**

## Situation and prediction

Picture a doorbell: a source, wires, a button and a device that needs energy. One wire from battery to lamp gives no continuous return path to the battery. Predict what changes when the button closes the gap. Draw the real objects in your notebook, then a simple diagram of their connections.

## New words, step by step

A **source** maintains an electrical potential difference and transfers energy to charges; here two AA cells in a holder supply roughly 3 V. A **conductor** provides a path for charge. A **load** receives electrical energy and turns it into light, heat or motion. A **switch** opens or closes the path. A **closed circuit** provides a continuous route from one source terminal, through the load, to the other. Closed does not mean short-circuited: a wire directly between source terminals bypasses the load and can dangerously heat the cells.

## Working through supplied data

In the teaching diagram, a 3 V source, switch and small lamp rated for 3 V are connected in series. With the switch open, current through the lamp is zero; closing it completes the route. Voltage alone cannot tell us brightness: the lamp and source characteristics matter. An LED needs a current-limiting resistor and correct polarity; it is not a drop-in substitute.

## A safe check

You may use a two-AA holder, a small 3 V lamp, wires and a switch. Connect with the source disconnected and have an adult check the layout. Never link the cell terminals directly, use a wall outlet, or open a cell. If you lack parts, draw both switch states.

## A reasoning trap

The mistaken picture says charge is used up in the lamp and does not return. Charge is conserved around a closed circuit; the source gives it energy and the lamp receives energy. Keep charge transfer separate from energy transfer.
